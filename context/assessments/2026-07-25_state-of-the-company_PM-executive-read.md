## §2 — How we got here, and what the method bought

The through-line is one discipline applied fractally: **never assert what you cannot prove, at every layer.** The code refuses to confirm without a state report. The bench refuses a vacuous PASS. The record refuses a commit without a census. The strategy refuses a claim without an artifact (the honest-claim floor), and the brand refuses a name without counsel's word. That coherence IS the company. It cost breadth — a clone-sprint would show fifty integrations where we have one deep and one designed — and the trade was correct: breadth is the moat the incumbents already own; honesty is the moat their economics (Big Tech) and their architecture (HA) cannot follow us into. The Rosonway evening is the method in miniature: a $30 hub test produced a frozen-surface defect nobody had seen, two standing laws, a stronger instrument, and a closed [S] row — because it ran instrument-first with predictions stated in advance. The system now reliably converts *operator evenings into permanent assets*, which is the compounding engine the whole strategy depends on.

**The second-order asset, named plainly:** we run the company the way the product runs a home — append-only record, honest verdicts, provable causality, decisions gated on evidence. That isomorphism is not cosmetic. It is why a solo founder can credibly claim infrastructure-grade discipline to a commercial buyer; it is the bus-factor insurance that makes the operation transmissible to a future teammate; and it will eventually be marketing copy with the rare property of being true.

## §3 — The risks, ranked as the PM actually sees them

1. **Solo-founder bandwidth against a multi-year ambition (risk-1; the big one, as ruled).** The AI-lane system multiplies desk hours, but every silicon act, ruling, and relationship routes through Nick's evenings — and post-launch those evenings acquire new competitors (support, community, interviews, pilots). **The FQ-3 trial is the single highest-leverage organizational decision available this year**; its ~October conversion review deserves gate-day seriousness (criteria pre-written — hold them like J1).
2. **The beachhead claim is ahead of the product** (the record's own words). Discovery de-risks the *direction*; org-hierarchy, RBAC, and signed audit export are real builds with real governance cost (a formal AMD, a Doc 09 amendment, `chain_hash` liveness), putting credible paying pilots in H1 2027, not 2026. Fine — but PRICED: the Nov-25 → first-revenue window is founder-capital runway, and the FQ-6 grant lane (SBIR/STTR-class) has external long-lead calendars — **a cheap cycle-deadline desk scan belongs early post-gate**, not when the runway feels short.
3. **Launch distribution.** The flag-plant lands in front of whatever audience exists on day one — currently zero by design. The mitigating asset: the content is genuinely novel (nobody else can publish receipts like "watch five commands fail honestly"). The launch-runway plan must treat audience-building as a producer with a deadline, not an afterthought.
4. **The standard race (watch tripwire T6)** — the highest-stakes external clock: a SCITT-profile or insurer-consortium move on building-event provenance degrades own-the-interface to fast-follow. The quarterly watch is the right instrument; the consolation is that the hard part (a reference implementation with a real evidence corpus) would still be ours.
5. **Naming/legal timing — well-instrumented, no longer top-tier:** every outcome of the ~Aug-5 read has a pre-ruled path (Architecture C ratified · the adverse branch = knockout+file · the suspension-clock frame · TSDR cadence). The only watch: never let brand-wait leak into launch-date slip (naming gates public surfaces, not code).
6. **Bus factor on the record itself:** the hivemind is Nick+AI-only today. If the trial converts, onboarding a second human INTO the record discipline (or a simplified interface onto it) becomes a real workstream — plan it, don't improvise it.
