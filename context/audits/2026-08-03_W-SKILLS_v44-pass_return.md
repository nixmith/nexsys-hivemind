## §5 — The finding that reframes the pass

### 5.1 The pass failed its stated target, and the reason is structural

The brief's Section 3 target was *"reduce always-loaded mass WITHOUT losing one censused rule."* Always-loaded mass went **up 710 tokens (+2.3%)**. I want to be direct about that rather than dress it up.

The reason is not that the compression was done badly. It is that **there is very little left to compress.** The 2026-07-28 and 2026-07-30 passes already removed the prose fat — that is what "17-in / 17-out, THE COMPRESSION" was. What remains in the mastheads is 54 (PM) and 33 (coder) named laws at close to minimal expression: an operative sentence and a pointer each, averaging 88 tokens for PM's arc-disciplines. There is no padding left to squeeze. Meanwhile the fold added five new laws by design, and the census record added its own mass.

Two measurements make this concrete, and both surprised me:

1. **Re-indexing prose into a named list COSTS tokens.** Measured twice: PM's `durable-disciplines` prose → D1–D18 cost **+139 tokens** for identical content; the coder's five prose arc blocks → one indexed list of 21 cost **+262 net of the changelog collapse**. Indexing buys addressability and census-ability — which is why I did it and would again — but the brief should stop describing the indexed form as a *compression* move. It is a **legibility** move that costs mass.
2. **The census convention is itself the accretion engine.** The brief's Section 3 instructs "prefix-prepend the pass record with the census arithmetic, per the prior-pass convention." My first-draft v44 records cost **2,274 always-loaded tokens** across three files. I measured them, cut them to **1,006**, and moved the narrative to this file. **A convention that prepends a paragraph of provenance to an always-loaded masthead every pass guarantees monotonic growth forever, in the one location where growth is most expensive.** Recommend the rule change in §6.1.

I am recording that plainly: **the fold, executed exactly as instructed, made the compaction survival profile slightly worse in the two files it enriched.** That is the trade the brief chose, and it was the right trade for durability of law — but it is a trade, and it should be made knowingly next time.

### 5.3 Why re-ordering does not fix it

The obvious cheap fix is to move the HTML-comment masthead to the *bottom* of each file, so the body claims the 5,000-token window. Costed:

| File | masthead-first (today) | masthead-last |
|---|---|---|
| PM | 0% of body survives; laws survive to D10 | 72% of body survives; **0 of 58 laws survive** |
| Coder | 31% of body | 93% of body; 0 of 33 conventions |
| Frontend | 39% of body | 54% of body; 0 masthead |

It trades one loss for the other. **Neither ordering works, because all three files are 1.7×–2.5× over the window.** Position engineering cannot fix a mass problem.

### 5.4 The only real fix — a tiering decision the hub owns

The three skills need to fit their critical content inside 5,000 tokens. For the PM that is a ~60% reduction, and the body alone (6,746) already exceeds the window, so it cannot come from the masthead alone. The shape that works:

1. **Always-loaded (~4,800 tokens):** the laws a session can violate *silently within its first act* — the state-pointer law, CI-is-the-gate-of-record, single-spine-writer, porcelain-not-word, chat-is-not-a-storage-tier, never-author-on-an-unmeasured-hop, returns-file-to-audits, the preflight gate and its STALE/CONFLICTED protocol, the deferred-gate blocking rule, *What You Never Do* — plus a compressed operating body.
2. **`references/arc-disciplines.md`** — the remaining indexed laws, conditionally loaded, with a hard trigger sentence in the body (*"Read this BEFORE authoring an instruction, auditing a return, or writing an operator block"*). The vendor guidance explicitly endorses conditional-load phrasing over generic pointers.
3. **Mechanics of §2 (three modes) and §3 (nine steps) → references.** They are procedures, read at a known moment, and they are the largest body blocks.

This is the return's §3A recommendation made executable and costed. **It needs a hub ruling because it changes what is guaranteed-present after compaction, and no lane should make that call unilaterally.** It is also a genuine project — one dedicated lane per skill, each with a full census — not a beat.

**One free mitigation available today, no ruling needed:** the vendor doc says *"If the skill is large or you invoked several others after it, re-invoke it after compaction to restore the full content."* A standing session rule — *after any auto-compaction, re-invoke your role skill before the next authoring act* — costs nothing and restores everything. Recommend folding it into the freshness preflight or the hub prompt.
