# RS-4 — The CRA Readiness Memo (applicability · the Article-14 procedure · the Dec-2027 map · the audit-trail thesis) — filed 2026-08-21

## §0 Falsifier headlines FIRST (what says "less applies than the hub assumes" or "more applies, sooner")

**F-1 · LESS, NOW: nothing in the Regulation binds us on 2026-09-11, because we are not yet a "manufacturer" of a product "made available on the market".** Article 14 binds *manufacturers* (Art. 14(1), (3)); a "manufacturer" is a person who develops products "and markets them under its name or trademark" (Art. 3(13)); "making available on the market" is "the supply of a product with digital elements for distribution or use on the Union market in the course of a commercial activity, whether in return for payment or free of charge" (Art. 3(22) — the "free of charge" tail is in the DEFINITION itself, served by the second mirror and missing from the first's rendering; re-fetch at intake); "placing on the market" is "the first making available" (Art. 3(21)) [MIRROR×2]. The Commission's guidance reads a standalone software product as placed on the market "when its manufacturing phase is complete and that software is first supplied for distribution or use on the EU market in the course of a commercial activity" (C(2026) 5252 Annex, point 14, p. 7) and says "a natural or legal person that shares free and open-source computer code on publicly accessible repositories is generally not considered to be placing that code on the EU market" (point 23, p. 9) [OFFICIAL via convenience PDF]. A `0.1.0-SNAPSHOT` / `0.1.0-skeleton` repository (`build.gradle.kts:7`, `distribution/VERSION:1`) whose LICENSE forbids use (`LICENSE:3–6`) is on neither reading "supplied for use". **The L-E sentence "the CRA process clock starting NOW" (L-E §3.1, line 150) is true for the field and false for us until a release is placed on the EU market.** The hub's I-5 routing ("the CRA Sept-11 clock routes to the compliance/launch-readiness track") stands — as a LAUNCH-readiness item, not a Sept-11 obligation. COUNSEL-FLAG C-1 (§1) marks the residual risk.

**F-2 · MORE, SOONER, on two triggers the company controls.** (i) **If the LICENSE stays proprietary at first release, a FREE release is probably still "commercial activity"**: Art. 3(22) itself says "whether in return for payment or free of charge"; the FOSS carve-out (recital (18) [OJ]; Art. 3(48)) needs a free and open-source licence; and the guidance's general test is "providing goods, in return for payment *or free of charge*, in a business-related context" (point 40, p. 16). A company with a trademark program and a product page releasing its flagship for free is a business-related context. Manufacturer obligations would then attach on the day of placing: Art. 14 from that day (if ≥ 2026-09-11), Annex I/conformity from 2027-12-11 or from the first substantial modification after it (Art. 69(2)–(3)). (ii) **Even under Apache-2.0, the Nabu-Casa-shaped plan is recital (15)'s monetisation example verbatim**: commercial activity is characterised "not only by charging a price … but also by charging a price for technical support services … by providing a software platform through which the manufacturer monetises other services, or by requiring as a condition for use the processing of personal data …" (recital (15) [OJ]). The day a paid cloud/sync/support service is sold *through* the engine, the free engine is placed on the market and the full manufacturer regime applies. (§1 rows (c)–(c′).)

**F-3 · The installed base is reportable even though it is not conformity-assessed.** Art. 69(2) shelters products placed before 2027-12-11 from the Regulation's requirements unless substantially modified after that date; Art. 69(3) carves Art. 14 OUT of that shelter — reporting duties apply to all products placed on the market before 2027-12-11 [MIRROR]. For continuously-updated software the shelter is thin anyway: "substantial modification" is any change "which affects the compliance … with the essential cybersecurity requirements" (Art. 3(30)); the guidance reads it as changes "introducing new threat vectors or materially changed attack scenarios" (HLC summary of C(2026) 5252 §4 [SECONDARY]). Plan for Dec-2027 as if every release after it is a new placing.

**F-4 · The L-E rows survive verification, with one upgrade and one hazard.** All Article-14 clocks and content banked by L-E (lines 148–150) and confirmed by the two-layer audit (rows 41–42) match the text on two independent mirrors (§2). **F-5 · A microenterprise is shielded from the panic clock but not from the duty.** Art. 64(10): no administrative fine "with regard to any failure to meet the deadline referred to in Article 14(2), point (a), or Article 14(4), point (a)" for microenterprises and small enterprises [MIRROR×2] — i.e., missing the 24-hour early warning is unfineable for us; the 72-hour notification and the final report are not so shielded. ## §2 Q2 — Article 14 as an operating procedure for a one-person manufacturer (applies from 2026-09-11 — to us only on the §1 (b′)/(c)/(c′) branches)

### §2.1 The duty, verified at text

**Trigger classes** (Art. 3 [MIRROR×2]; Art. 14(5) [MIRROR×2]):
- (42) **actively exploited vulnerability** — "a vulnerability for which there is reliable evidence that a malicious actor has exploited it in a system without permission of the system owner". Note the three-part test: reliable evidence · a malicious actor · exploitation without permission. A CVE in a dependency is not by itself an *actively exploited* vulnerability *in our product*; it is one when there is reliable evidence of exploitation of our product (or, per guidance section 9 as summarised by HLC [SECONDARY], when a vulnerability becomes "known" through public databases, coordinated disclosure, internal testing, or "prominent reporting in reliable media" AND its applicability to our product is validated — §9 lists §9.1 as not read directly).
- (44) **incident having an impact on the security of the product** — "an incident that negatively affects or is capable of negatively affecting the ability of a product … to protect the availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of data or functions"; (43) "incident" and (45) "near miss" are imported from Directive (EU) 2022/2555 Art. 6(6)/(5).
- **severe** (Art. 14(5)): an incident is severe where "(a) it negatively affects or is capable of negatively affecting the ability of a product with digital elements to protect the availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of sensitive or important data or functions; or (b) it has led or is capable of leading to the introduction or execution of malicious code in a product with digital elements or in the network and information systems of a user of the product".

**Clocks and content** (Art. 14(2), (4) [MIRROR×2] — matches L-E line 148 exactly):

**User-notification duty** (Art. 14(8) [MIRROR×2]): after becoming aware of an actively exploited vulnerability or a severe incident, "the manufacturer shall inform the impacted users of the product with digital elements, and where appropriate all users, of that vulnerability or incident and, where necessary, of any risk mitigation and corrective measures that users can deploy", "without undue delay", in a structured, machine-readable format where appropriate; if the manufacturer fails, the CSIRT may inform users itself. This duty needs a channel that exists at launch (§2.3).

**Voluntary path** (Art. 15 [MIRROR]): anyone may notify vulnerabilities and cyber threats (15(1)) and incidents/near misses (15(2)); the CSIRT may deprioritise voluntary reports (15(3)); if a non-manufacturer reports an actively exploited vulnerability in our product, the CSIRT "shall without undue delay inform the manufacturer" (15(4)) — i.e., OTHER people's voluntary reports can start OUR clock; confidentiality and no-additional-obligations (15(5)). Art. 17(4): notifying "shall not subject the notifying natural or legal person to increased liability"; Art. 17(5): ENISA adds fixed vulnerabilities to the European vulnerability database (EUVD) with the manufacturer's agreement.

**Awareness is the clock's origin, not platform availability.** Every clock runs from "becoming aware"; ENISA's FAQ defers the meaning to Commission guidance §9.1 (not read — §9). Design consequence: the runbook's first line is a *timestamped awareness record*, and the event log is the natural place for it (§5).

### §2.2 RUNBOOK DRAFT — "Article-14-grade response", v0, dated 2026-09-11 (one page; readiness-rail language; applies on the §1 branches where we are a manufacturer, and is the voluntary-report playbook on the others)

**Roles.** *Reporter-1* = the founder (decides reportability; files). *Reporter-2* = a named backup with EU Login (required because a 24-hour clock crosses sleep; ENISA registers representatives by EU Login). *Counsel* = consulted on the reportability call when the facts are ambiguous, never a blocker on the 24-hour early warning (Art. 64(10)(a) removes the fine for a missed 24-hour deadline for a microenterprise, not the duty).

**Step 0 — Awareness record (T0).** The moment a credible signal lands (security mailbox, GitHub private advisory, CSIRT contact under Art. 15(4), a user report, a media report, internal testing), write ONE line to the incident file: UTC timestamp · source · one-sentence claim · affected versions as claimed. This timestamp is "becoming aware" for every clock below. Do not debate whether it is "reliable evidence" before writing it; debate after.

**Step 1 — Reportability decision (≤ 12 h from T0).** Ask, in order: (1) Is it OUR product (the engine, the dashboard, the `.deb`, a bundled dependency as shipped)? If a dependency only: Art. 13(6) — report to the component's maintainer; still ours if exploited *in our product*. (2) Is there *reliable evidence* of *exploitation* by a *malicious actor* *without permission* (Art. 3(42))? If yes → CLASS V (actively exploited vulnerability). (3) Else, is there an *incident* that negatively affects (or could) availability/authenticity/integrity/confidentiality of *sensitive or important* data or functions, or led (or could lead) to malicious-code execution in the product or the user's systems (Art. 14(5))? If yes → CLASS I (severe incident). (4) Else → NOT mandatory; consider Art. 15 voluntary notification; open a normal advisory. Record the decision and the paragraph relied on in the incident file.

**Step 2 — Artifacts pulled (by T0 + 12 h, in parallel with Step 1).** (a) Affected version range: the `VERSION` strings shipped (`/opt/homesynapse/VERSION`), the `SystemStartedEvent.version` values seen in the reporter's own bench/test logs, and the release tags. (b) The SBOM for each affected version (once §8 lands it; until then, `./gradlew dependencies` output + the dashboard's `package-lock.json`, pinned to the tag). (c) The event-log excerpt around the incident from any affected deployment the user consents to share: `global_position` range, `ingest_time`, `event_type`, `actor_ref`, `causal_context`, `categories` (these envelope fields exist today: `core/event-model/MODULE_CONTEXT.md:43`). (d) Exploit/indicator description as received. (e) Member States where the product is known to have been made available (for a free download: the users' Member State determination — C-7).

**Step 3 — Early warning (≤ 24 h from T0).** File on the Single Reporting Platform to our coordinator CSIRT (Art. 14(7) order; for a no-establishment, no-AR, no-importer free download → the Member State with the most users): notification type (vulnerability/incident) · level = early warning · reporter · manufacturer name · product · title · for V: CVE if any (EUVD optional) · for I: suspected unlawful/malicious act? · Member States concerned. Paste the awareness timestamp. If the platform is unreachable, email the CSIRT's published end-point and keep the send receipt (the platform's own FAQ will name the end-points — §2.3 item 4).

**Step 4 — Notification (≤ 72 h from T0).** Update the filing with: general product information · general nature of the exploit and vulnerability (V) or nature of the incident + initial assessment (I) · corrective/mitigating measures TAKEN · measures USERS CAN TAKE (for us today that is: rotate the bearer token via mint-new + revoke-old — `api/rest-api/MODULE_CONTEXT.md:162`; keep the loopback bind; apply the update once published) · sensitivity indication.

**Step 5 — Users (without undue delay after Step 1).** Publish the advisory on the advisory page (§2.3 item 2) in a machine-readable form (CSAF/OSV-style JSON beside the human text — the Regulation says "structured, machine-readable format where appropriate"); push the same text to the in-dashboard notice channel when one exists (today none — §8). Art. 14(8) wants *impacted* users informed and, where appropriate, *all* users: for a product with no phone-home, "all users" = the public advisory + the package-feed notice.

**Step 6 — Fix + secure distribution.** Ship the security update as a separate release from functionality changes where feasible (Annex I II(2)); signed (§8); free of charge "accompanied by advisory messages" (Annex I II(8)). Record the corrective-measure availability date in the incident file — it starts the 14-day final-report clock (V).

**Step 7 — Final report (V: ≤ 14 days after the measure is available; I: ≤ 1 month after the notification).** Description + severity + impact · (V) malicious-actor information where available · (V) details of the security update/corrective measures · (I) type of threat or root cause · (I) applied and ongoing mitigation. Attach the log-derived incident sequence where the user consented. Close the incident file with the final-report submission receipt; add the vulnerability to the public disclosure page (Annex I II(4)); agree EUVD publication with ENISA if asked (Art. 17(5)).

**Step 8 — Intermediate reports** on CSIRT request (Art. 14(6)); **retention**: keep the incident file 10 years with the technical documentation (Art. 13(13)).

## §4 Q4 — Classification + conformity route + the standards landscape (dated)

**Classification — the reading.** A general-purpose home-automation engine that *coordinates* locks, cameras and alarms without *being* one is a **default-category product** on the text and on the Commission's technical descriptions:
- Annex III Class I item 17 is "Smart home products with security functionalities, including smart door locks, security cameras, baby monitoring systems and alarm systems" [MIRROR]; Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392 (28 Nov 2025) describes them as "Products with digital elements that protect the physical security of consumers in a residential setting and which can be controlled or managed remotely from other systems" [OJ]. The engine does not itself protect physical security; it is the "other system" from which such products are controlled. Its recital (3): a product is subject to the important-product procedures "if that product as a whole is an important or critical product" — integrating components of those categories does not reclassify the host [OJ]; Art. 7(1) says the same for integration [MIRROR].
- Item 16 "Smart home general purpose virtual assistants" — described as products "that communicate on the public Internet … that process demands, tasks or questions based on natural language prompts … and provide access to other services or control connected devices" [OJ] — does NOT describe the engine as packaged (no natural-language surface; no public-Internet communication). It WOULD describe an LLM-agent surface added to the engine that takes natural-language prompts and controls devices, if it talks to the public Internet (e.g., a hosted model). **Tripwire F-7(b).**
- Item 11 "Operating systems" — the engine is not an OS by the Implementing Regulation's description (F-7(a)); the marketing word is the only exposure. A full SD-card OS image would be a different product.
- Items 1 (identity management), 5 (VPN), 7 (SIEM — "collect data from multiple sources, analyse and correlate … for security-related purposes" [OJ]), 12 (routers): not the engine's core functionality; note that a *security-monitoring* positioning of the event log ("your home's SIEM") would invite item 7 — language discipline, not architecture.
- Annex IV (critical): hardware security modules, smart meter gateways, smartcards — N/A.
- Art. 7(2)(b)'s criterion ("a function which carries a significant risk of adverse effects … through direct manipulation, such as a central system function") is the Commission's *power to add* categories by delegated act with a 12-month transition (Art. 7(3)) — a hub category could be added; watch item, not a present obligation. COUNSEL-FLAG C-8 (MED): confirm the default-category reading in writing, given that the product's intended purpose (Annex II (4)) will mention controlling locks/alarms.

**Conformity route.** Default category → Art. 32(1): the manufacturer chooses (a) internal control, **Module A** (Annex VIII Part I), (b) Module B + C, (c) Module H, or (d) a European cybersecurity certification scheme under Art. 27(9) [MIRROR×2]. - **GDPR.** Art. 2(2)(c) exempts "the processing of personal data by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity"; recital 18's last sentence: "this Regulation applies to controllers or processors which provide the means for processing personal data for such personal or household activities" [OJ].
