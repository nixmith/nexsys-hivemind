The decision rule was given; **the navigator did not ask for and does not hold the answer**, that being health data and outside this session entirely. 5. **The tax-ID fork** — NFCU's public page offers single-member LLCs the owner's SSN in place of the EIN. §2.3 rules EIN. ## §E — Material account terms discovered (risk-register candidates)

Read in full from NFCU's own published Business Solutions Disclosure Booklet and Schedule of Fees and Charges, both provided by Nick. These are the terms with consequences beyond the application, surfaced here because they bear on FIN-1 and on the liability posture.

1. **Personal liability for negative balances.** The business member **and the owner** agree to be jointly and severally liable for negative balances on any account in which either holds an ownership interest, including overdrafts, *regardless of the cause*, plus collection costs and attorney's fees. **This is a narrow but real carve-out past the LLC shield.** Operating consequence: the account must never go negative, and should carry a buffer above the largest recurring debit.
2. **Personal-account cross-default.** The owner's personal accounts must remain in good standing **for the duration of the business relationship**; failure to maintain that standing beyond a stated period may result in restriction or closure of the associated business accounts. **This makes personal financial standing a single point of failure for the company's operating account.** Recorded as an argument for a second business banking relationship as continuity redundancy — *flagged, not developed*, per the §2.4 boundary.
3. **Unauthorized ACH debits may be unrecoverable if not reported within one banking day of posting.** Business accounts are not covered by the consumer electronic-transfer protections that would otherwise give a long dispute window. **This is the single largest operational difference from a personal account.** Countermeasures agreed at §C.2: alerts on every debit, daily business-day review, statements never suppressed.
4. **The bank contractually forbids personal use of business accounts.** Strategy §3.5's hygiene law now has a mirror running the other direction: commingling is not only a liability-shield problem, it is a breach of the account agreement.
5. **The share account must be maintained.** A sustained shortfall below one share beyond a stated period may close the membership savings account, **which terminates the business membership**. A forgotten, emptied share account is a silent failure mode.
6. **No payable-on-death designation applies to business accounts.** Succession runs entirely through the Operating Agreement and the estate. **Whether the OA carries a succession provision is worth confirming** — a separate act, noted only.
7. **No arbitration clause and no class-action waiver.** What stands in their place is a notice-and-cure provision requiring notice and a reasonable opportunity to correct before either party litigates. **Materially more favourable than the industry norm; banked as a positive.**
8. **A foreign-transaction percentage applies to internet purchases from merchants that process abroad** — which describes a meaningful share of developer tooling, hosting, and subscription spend. It will accrue quietly against the card line.
9. **NFCU does not provide notary services in Louisiana.** Relevant only if a trade-name filing is ever made, since Louisiana requires notarisation for it.
