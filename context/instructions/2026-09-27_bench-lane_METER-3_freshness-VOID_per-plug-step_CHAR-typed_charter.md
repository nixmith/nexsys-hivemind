## §1 The decisions (settled — D-v81-17; the lane implements, and raises a fork only with evidence)
1. **The freshness VOID.** `field_within` gains an optional `fresh_within_s: "${C.metering.fresh-within-s.<plug>}"`. At the read, `age = read_at − witness` (both already recorded). If `fresh_within_s` is set and `age > fresh_within_s`: the row's verdict is **VOID** with `reason: "stale witness: age <age> s > <fresh_within_s> s"`, recorded (the run continues; the close counts it as not-WITHIN exactly as OUTSIDE is counted today — read how `on_outside: record` reaches the verdict and give VOID the same path). The ratio and deviation are still computed and recorded (the datum is not thrown away; its verdict is). Without the key, behavior is byte-identical to today (every existing test green by construction — say so in the return).
2. **The per-plug windows and biases, minted from the CHAR bundle** (constants `metering:`): `fresh-within-s: { g4-1: 30, tr3: 180, g4-2: 30 }` — the Gen4 reported every ≈ 5 s under load (the store: `state_reported` every 5 s, 22:33:02 → 22:34:31Z; `reporting_configured … clusters=3 verified=3` at adoption, ActivePower min 5 s), 6× = 30; the TR3's reports were 87–165 s apart under load and ≈ 66 s at idle (the b5 audit §1), 3× the longest ≈ 180. `bias-pct: { g4-1: 2.3, tr3: 8.0, g4-2: 2.9 }` — the unrounded-read ratios (G4-1 2.19 / 2.44 on the 42-W reads; the TR3 7.1–8.9; G4-2's one fresh rep 2.94). Each key carries a `provenance:` row naming the bundle `metering-known-load-20260926T225552Z` and the audit. The lane copies the numbers from the audit; it does not re-derive them (a re-mint's numbers are the record's).
3. **The load step per plug.** The REP prompts read "LAMP out of <PLUG> for at least ${C.metering.step-s.<plug>} seconds, then back in" with `step-s: { g4-1: 15, tr3: 90, g4-2: 15 }` (≥ the plug's cadence so the 0 W and the return are both reported); the wait afterwards: "until `power_w` in the meter-reader window shows the lit value AND its report is newer than the step" — plain words; the runner's VOID is the check.
4. **CHAR typed (IR-58).** CHAR-BEFORE's `confirm: enter` is replaced by FOUR `let:` entries typed at its end — `char_before_b1_offset_w` (the mean of B1 − A over the three pairs, signed), `char_before_b1_spread_w` (max − min of B1 − A), `char_before_b2_offset_w`, `char_before_b2_spread_w` (`type: number`, no `min:`; each prompt says exactly what to subtract and what to type); CHAR-AFTER's `char_after_readings` (the count) is replaced by the same four with `char_after_` — 22 − 1 + 8 = **29 entries**, BM1b T4's "22 in order" pin re-cut to 29 with the new names in order; the scripted keyboard in T3 gains eight numbers. The paper sheet stays the witness for the raw pairs (the prompts say so).
5. **The bias beside the verdict (IR-62).** The REP line and the receipt gain `bias_pct` (from the constants) and `corrected_ratio = ratio / (1 + bias_pct/100)` — RECORDED, never the verdict's (the band stays the guaranteed figure; whether a biased plug carries an accuracy-bearing load is Nick's `BIAS: tolerate|exclude`, decided at the packet, not here).
6. **BH-2.** `boot-health.yaml`'s `forbidden:` gains `zigbee.permit_join_opened` — a window at a boot the scenario did not declare turns boot-health red. (The T1/T1b flows open windows deliberately and never run boot-health in the same boot; if the lane finds a scenario that does, it names it in §5 and does not add the token.)
