### 2.2 The launch narrative, in the only form the language law permits

**One statement, three moves. This is the spine every artifact inherits.**

> **Move 1 — the grievance, in the users' own construction.** Your home tells you it did something. It is telling you it *sent a command*. The lock may or may not have locked; the light may or may not be on; the platform will show you a confident state either way, and if you ask it tomorrow why, the answer is gone.
>
> **Move 2 — the missing layer, stated as missing.** The instruments the field has are the right instruments — retries, availability heuristics, better integrations, better models. Every one of them is necessary. **What is missing beneath them is a categorical floor: a single point where what actually happened is recorded, in order, and cannot be quietly edited afterward.** A probabilistic improvement with no categorical floor beneath it is not a guarantee; it is a lowered error rate. **The floor is missing from the field — it is not superior to what the field has.**
>
> **Move 3 — the three questions.** *Why did it fire? Why didn't it fire? Did it actually happen?* Answered from a complete record that is never trimmed, on hardware you own, with the internet unplugged.

### 2.3 The named launch artifacts

#### (a) The README front-door

**FACT — HIGH.** GitHub's own guidance: a README "is often the first item a visitor will see," and typically covers *what the project does · why the project is useful · how users can get started · where users can get help · who maintains and contributes.* ([GitHub Docs, About READMEs, accessed 2026-08-01](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/about-readmes) — **the page carries no visible publication date; treat as undated-but-authoritative.**)

**FACT — HIGH — the structural gap the literature names.** Prana, Treude, Thung, Atapattu & Lo, *"Categorizing the Content of GitHub README Files,"* **Empirical Software Engineering, online 2018-10-12**, 4,226 manually annotated sections from 393 repositories: *"Information discussing the 'What' and 'How' of a repository is very common, while many README files lack information regarding the purpose and status of a repository."* ([link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-018-9660-3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-018-9660-3), accessed 2026-08-01)

**INFERENCE — HIGH.** ***Why* and *status* are the scarce categories — and they are exactly the two a truth-brand needs in the first screen.** Most READMEs are all what-and-how. Our differentiator is a *why* (the missing floor) and a *status* (what is real today versus designed-for). The north star already models the status discipline in its own "Current state (honestly)" section, and its frontmatter records the hub softening one claim that was designed-for rather than true-today. **That discipline is the README's most transferable asset and it should be visible above the fold, not buried at the bottom.**

**FACT — HIGH — what is table stakes versus what still differentiates, on 2026 data.** Hora, Montandon & Costa, *"What's Inside a GitHub Repository? An Empirical Study on the Contents of 10K Projects,"* **arXiv:2605.16701, submitted 2026-05-15** ([arxiv.org/html/2605.16701](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16701), accessed 2026-08-01): README.md **95.3%** · `docs/` **37.4%** · CONTRIBUTING.md **31.7%** · CHANGELOG.md **27.9%** · CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md **16.2%** · **SECURITY.md 13.5%**.

**INFERENCE — MED-HIGH.** A README is table stakes at 95.3%. **SECURITY.md at 13.5% and CHANGELOG.md at 27.9% are minority signals that specifically read as "this project tells you the truth about its own state"** — which is the brand. X-2 already requires SECURITY.md; this is the first evidence that it is also *positioning*, not just hygiene.

### 3.5 Two operational holes this lane found

**(a) The `security@` trap. FACT — HIGH (internal) → INFERENCE — HIGH.** The launch-readiness checklist's Hours 4–24 block creates `nick@`, `hello@`, `legal@` **plus a catch-all**. ### 4.3 The first-contributor funnel — where the first ten plausibly come from

**FACT — HIGH.** The largest dated empirical study of casual contributors found they are *"48.98% of the whole population of contributors in the projects analyzed"* but *"responsible for only 1.73% of the total number of commits."* What they did: **28.64%** typo/grammar fixes, **30.20%** bug fixes, **18.75%** feature additions, **8.85%** refactoring. **The dominant motivation was self-interest as a user** — *"scratch their own itch"* was named by **90 of 197** surveyed casual contributors. (Pinto, Steinmacher & Gerosa, *"More Common Than You Think: An In-Depth Study of Casual Contributors,"* SANER 2016, [ime.usp.br/~gerosa/papers/saner2016.pdf](https://www.ime.usp.br/~gerosa/papers/saner2016.pdf), accessed 2026-08-01. **Dating caveat, MED-HIGH: this is a 2015–2016 source and no post-2023 replication was found.**)

**FACT — MED-HIGH.** A named maintainer's survey of 20 non-employer maintainers on a large project: *"most came to the project as their businesses were migrating… often because their employer had business needs."* The same maintainer documents an explicit issue-reporter → contributor **tactic** — replying *"Thank you for reporting this issue! Looks like a bug. Would you like to help?"* and asking the reporter to write a failing test or docs. (Doubrovkine, **2024-12-14**, [code.dblock.org/2024/12/14/how-to-grow-open-source-contributors-and-maintainers.html](https://code.dblock.org/2024/12/14/how-to-grow-open-source-contributors-and-maintainers.html), accessed 2026-08-01.)

**FACT — HIGH, reported as an absence and it changes the plan.** **No dated source states an issue-reporter → PR-author conversion rate.** Only the tactic exists, unmeasured. **No number is supplied here.**

**INFERENCE — MED-HIGH — the funnel, honestly.** **Our first ten external contributors will overwhelmingly be users who hit friction with their own hardware** — not readers of an announcement. Both dated sources point the same way (itch-driven, user-first), neither measures announcement-channel attribution, so the confidence is capped. **This maps exactly onto the ruled day-1 surface:** a device-profile submission *is* a scratch-your-own-itch act by a user whose device is not yet fully characterised. **A-5's on-ramp is not merely the correct first surface — it is the only surface the evidenced funnel actually flows through.**

**The concrete funnel, in order:**

1. **A user installs, and one of their devices is generically served but not characterised.** *(The additive-not-gating rule makes this a nuisance rather than a brick — which is the entire point of it, and the reason it belongs at the readiness bar rather than in a backlog.)*
2. **They look for how to fix it for themselves.** → The profile-submission how-to (P-4). **Zero-review side-load first**: they edit their own override file and their device works **today**, with no merge involved.
3. **They open a Discussion or an issue with the profile they wrote.** *(This is the conversion moment, and it is the moment §4.4's engagement lever applies.)*
4. **It merges at the same gate as machine ingest** and their name is in NOTICE. *(Provenance recordable — L-B §7.2 row 1.3.)*
5. **They come back with a second device.** *(The return-contributor signal, §D-5.)*

**INFERENCE — HIGH.** Steps 2 and 3 are separated by a **config key** (L-B §7.2 row 1.1) and step 3→4 by **a published format** (L-B §1.6). **Both are already-sized, gate-independent engineering rows.** The presence plan cannot substitute for them: **no amount of narrative moves someone from step 1 to step 2 if the override file has no config key to point at.**

### 4.4 What the evidence says actually converts — and what does not

**FACT — HIGH — the retention lever, from the largest recent study.** *"The First Issue Matters: Linking Task-Level Characteristics to Long-Term Newcomer Retention in OSS,"* **arXiv:2603.27136v1, dated 2026-03-28**, **45,865 first-issue samples across 777 GitHub repositories** ([arxiv.org/html/2603.27136](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27136), accessed 2026-08-01):

- **"Maintainer comments exhibit a stronger positive effect on retention potential,"** improving *"mostly monotonic[ally]"* with each additional maintainer engagement.
- Discussion volume is non-linear: **1–3 comments show "a friction dip"; ~5–15 comments produce "a significant enrichment climb"; beyond 15, retention declines.**
- **Sentiment: retention is highest for *neutral* discussions and lower for strongly positive and very negative ones.**
- Issues reported by **moderate-experience** contributors retain best. Model AUC 0.650 → 0.771.
- **The paper reports no good-first-issue label effect and no response-latency effect.**

**INFERENCE — HIGH — and it is a direct hit on our voice.** *Neutral* sentiment outperforming *strongly positive* is the evidenced version of **Register C** (the ruled UI microcopy voice: *"never blames, never cheerful, never apologetic"*) applied to issue threads. **The enthusiastic-maintainer welcome is not the retention move; the substantive, plain, several-turn exchange is.** That is a voice we already have written down.

**FACT — HIGH — the good-first-issue collapse.** *"A Longitudinal Analysis of Good First Issue Practices and Newcomer Pull Requests in Popular OSS Projects,"* arXiv:2604.27532v2, 406,826 issues and 1,117 newcomer GFI PRs across 37 repositories ([arxiv.org/html/2604.27532v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27532v2), accessed 2026-08-01): the GFI ratio fell **0.91% → 0.63%** from January 2024; newcomer engagement held at **~27%**; **the merge rate of newcomer GFI PRs fell from 61.9% to 42.2%**; bug-fix tasks merge best at **68.7%**; and decisively — **"initial PR characteristics showed no significant association with merge outcomes,"** despite newcomer PR descriptions growing **57%** longer. *(Verified independently by both sub-lanes.)*

**INFERENCE — MED-HIGH.** **Newcomer PR polish does not predict acceptance.** Effort spent coaching submission quality is poorly targeted; effort spent on **task selection and maintainer engagement** is well targeted.

**FACT — MED-HIGH — the null result on the documents everyone starts with.** Gaughan, Champion, Hwang & Shaw, *"The Introduction of README and CONTRIBUTING Files in Open Source Software Development,"* arXiv:2502.18440v1 (data through **2024-03-16**, posted **Feb 2025**): *"Given sparse data for new contributors, we did not find any significant relationships between document traits and contributor recruitment."* The authors also note these files are typically published **reactively** — *"often published later, following a flurry of contributions"* — and that *"1658 README files (39% of our dataset) would take less than 10 seconds to read."* ([arxiv.org/html/2502.18440v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18440v1), accessed 2026-08-01.)

**INFERENCE — MED-HIGH, and stated carefully.** This is a **null due to sparse data, not a measured null.** Read it as *"no recruitment effect was detectable,"* **never** as *"CONTRIBUTING.md is useless."* The operational consequence: **treat CONTRIBUTING.md as hygiene and as a filter, not as an acquisition lever** — and do not let its authorship displace the config key (§4.3 step 2), which is the thing the funnel actually depends on.

**FACT — HIGH, reported as an absence (§0.3 limit 8b).** **No dated source supplies a first-response-latency → newcomer-returns effect size.** The dedicated latency paper (arXiv:2311.07786v2, IEEE TSE 2024) asserts the mechanism in prose only — *"delayed responses are often perceived as negligence, increasing the risk of the contributor abandoning the PR"* — with **no quantitative link.** CHAOSS asserts the same and **cites no numeric study.** **§D-5 therefore justifies the metric on convergence and controllability, and any effect-size claim in charter copy would be fabricated.**

**The consolidated funnel rule (OPINION — HIGH):**

> **Seed at most ~5 good-first-issues, all bug-fix-shaped, each with a reproduction; never let one go stale. But treat the device-profile channel as the real good-first-issue substitute** — it is pattern-matched against a corpus rather than judged (L-B §1.2), which is why its review does not queue the way a curation review does. **And spend the engagement budget in the first thread, several substantive neutral turns deep, because that is the only lever the 2026 evidence actually measures.**

## D-5 — MEASUREMENT + REVIEW CADENCE

### 5.1 Why stars are not merely vanity — they are a liability channel

**FACT — HIGH.** *"Six Million (Suspected) Fake Stars in GitHub: A Growing Spiral of Popularity Contests, Spams, and Malware,"* arXiv:2412.13459 (**v1 2024-12-18 as "4.5 Million," v2 2025-09-06 as "Six Million"**): *"fake-star-related activities have rapidly surged in 2024"*; the majority *"are used to promote short-lived phishing malware repositories"*; and the load-bearing sentence — **"fake stars only have a promotion effect in the short term (i.e., less than two months) and become a liability in the long term."** ([arxiv.org/abs/2412.13459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13459) · tool at [github.com/hehao98/StarScout](https://github.com/hehao98/StarScout), accessed 2026-08-01.)

**FACT — MED-HIGH, independently.** HN score explains only ~8% of star variance (r = 0.29), and *"each HN upvote converts to roughly 1.4 GitHub stars within 48 hours"* — so the star count is substantially a measurement of one 24-hour event we did not schedule. (King, 2026-04-23, above.)

**INFERENCE — HIGH.** Stars are (a) purchasable, (b) known to degrade standing when bought, (c) weakly coupled to the launch we do not control, and (d) **a metric a truth brand should be conspicuously uninterested in.** **REC: do not track stars, do not cite stars, and say once in the README that we do not.** That last clause is free positioning.

### 5.2 The seven weekly numbers

**Sourcing note, stated because the brief demands it and the evidence is thinner than it looks:** four of the seven are **named CHAOSS metrics with published definitions**. The justification for tracking them is **peer convergence and controllability**, never a measured effect size — **§0.3 limit 8b: no dated study supplies a latency→retention effect, and CHAOSS itself cites none.** Any charter copy claiming one would be fabricated.

([chaoss.community/kb/metric-time-to-first-response/](https://www.chaoss.community/kb/metric-time-to-first-response/), page updated **2024-10-24**; model page updated **2023-02-16**) | **The only metric fully under a solo maintainer's control on day one**, and the only one CHAOSS attaches a published threshold to | **Median + worst**, this week |

### 5.3 The review cadence — and the reshape that makes it survivable

**OPINION — HIGH, and it is the same argument A-7 already accepted one layer down.** A weekly human review is a standing weekly human duty. **Nobody can own a standing weekly human duty on an unquantified attended-hours budget** — that is L-B §3.2's finding and A-7's ruling about C-3. **The identical logic applies to the review itself.**

> **REC: the seven weekly numbers are a GENERATED artifact, not a human report.** A scheduled job emits them; **the human act is reading them, not producing them.** If M-1…M-7 cannot be generated, **track fewer** — the failure mode is a review ritual that lapses and takes the discipline with it.

### 5.4 The adjust / abort triggers

**FACT — HIGH.** Syncthing discontinued its Android app, **2024-10-20**, stating: *"The app saw no significant development for a long time and without Play releases I do no longer see enough benefit and/or have enough motivation to keep up the ongoing maintenance an app requires even without doing much, if any, changes,"* citing *"Google making Play publishing something between hard and impossible."* ([forum.syncthing.net/t/discontinuing-syncthing-android/23002](https://forum.syncthing.net/t/discontinuing-syncthing-android/23002), accessed 2026-08-01.) **FACT — HIGH.** Automattic cut sponsored WordPress contribution to *"about 45 hours a week"* on **2025-01-09**, citing ecosystem imbalance and litigation costs ([automattic.com/2025/01/09/…](https://automattic.com/2025/01/09/aligning-automattics-sponsored-contributions-to-wordpress/)). Plus Gentoo (2024-04-18) and the Hacktoberfest 2020 mid-event rule change, both above.

**INFERENCE — MED-HIGH — the transferable rule.** **Every verified example names a concrete, externally-caused trigger and drops a SURFACE, not the project.** None says "I got tired." **The publicly-defensible abort is the one with a named external cause and a named narrower scope — so ours must be pre-written that way, before we need them.** Syncthing is the closest domain analogue found: a self-hosted infrastructure project dropping one surface, with distribution friction plus motivation decay as the stated cause. *(**Absence, reported:** no dated abort/adjust example from the smart-home ecosystem specifically was found — searches across four named projects returned only changelogs.)*
