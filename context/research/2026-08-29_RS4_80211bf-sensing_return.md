# RS-4 — 802.11bf physics · sensing consent · the Annex I close-out (filed 2026-08-29 CT)

## §0 The falsifier findings first (H10 protocol)

**Read this section as: what the baselines said, what broke, what merely bent, and what held. Six findings; three of them change the shape of a standing sentence.**

**F-1 — "No consent framework anywhere" is BROKEN as a universal and HOLDS as a Wi-Fi/home statement.** The RS-3 and L-E baselines carried the sensing-consent vacuum as absolute. It is not. Two cellular-side bodies have written the vocabulary: ETSI's 6G ISAC group defines the term — *"Sensing consent is a form of authorization, where appropriate, given with the knowledge of the data subject for the collection and processing of PII during sensing activities"* (clause 4.6.2) — and writes a potential requirement, *"The 6GS should offer mechanisms to exercise choice and obtain consent from human involved in sensing activities and connected to the network, while also providing transparency information"* (PR 5.7-1), while naming the non-participant directly: *"It is even possible to sense humans that are not connected to the 6GS. This feature has implications on privacy, since people can be sensed without knowing that they are sensed, by whom they are sensed, and for which purpose the sensing result is used"* (5.8.1) ([VERIFIED] ETSI GR ISC 004 V1.1.1, 2026-02, parent re-fetch). 3GPP's Rel-19 ISAC service requirements gate sensing on consent and acknowledge the same person: *"The introduction of sensing capabilities can enable tracking of people and objects in the environment, including people not carrying UEs. Thus, additional considerations are needed to protect their rights to privacy"* (4.3); *"Subject to regulation and user consent, the 5G network may be able to link sensing results with 3GPP subscriber identity"* (5.2.4) ([VERIFIED] ETSI TS 122 137 V19.1.0, 2026-01, parent re-fetch). Both are cellular, one is a non-normative Group Report, and in both the consent principal is the *subscriber*; the non-connected person gets an acknowledgement, not a mechanism. **For Wi-Fi in homes the vacuum is intact and now enumerated** (§2.1): IEEE 802.11bf, the Wi-Fi Alliance, the IETF, CSA/Matter, the FCC, the FTC, the ICO, the EU institutions, every named civil-society body, and every shipping Wi-Fi-sensing vendor carry no consent semantics. The corrected sentence is: *the vacuum is Wi-Fi-specific and home-specific; the vocabulary exists next door in 6G ISAC and has not crossed.* That is a better wedge, not a worse one — it means the layer can borrow terms that already have a standards pedigree (§2.4).

### 1.2 What the amendment actually standardizes (PHY/MAC) — and what it deliberately does not

At the NIST authors' overview (Ropitault, da Silva, Blandino, Sahoo, Golmie et al., IEEE Communications Magazine; these are TGbf contributors) — parent re-fetched:

- **Phases.** *"Sensing session setup, sensing measurement setup, sensing measurement instance(s), sensing measurement setup termination, and sensing session termination."*
- **Roles.** Initiator/responder (who starts and who participates) and transmitter/receiver (who sends the PPDUs and who measures) are separate axes ([VERIFIED]).
- **Two sub-7 GHz variants.** *"Trigger-Based (TB)… the sensing initiator is an AP, and one or more clients assume the role of sensing responders"*; *"Non-TB… the sensing initiator is a client, and only one STA (an AP) assumes the role of sensing responder"*; *"the TB variant is the only one allowing multistatic sensing"* ([VERIFIED]). Sensing-by-proxy (SBP) lets a non-AP STA ask the AP to run TB sensing on its behalf ([VERIFIED] NIST; Du et al.).
- **60 GHz (DMG) sensing** is a separate procedure family — monostatic, bistatic, multistatic, coordinated, passive — assuming beamforming training is already done ([VERIFIED] Du et al. ar5iv 2207.04859; Blandino et al. NIST).
- **The measurement is CSI and only CSI.** *"CSI is the only sensing measurement considered. Thus, it is also the only report."* Report compression: *"Sensing measurements are encoded into 8 or 10 bits, and only 1 out of 4, 8, or 16 subcarrier values are reported (in a process termed grouping)."* ([VERIFIED] NIST, parent re-fetch). Pre-final candidates (full CSI, partial CSI, truncated PDP/CIR, frequency-domain differential quantization) are recorded in Du et al. ([VERIFIED]).
- **Negotiation and refusal exist at the protocol level.** The responder *"either accepts/rejects the request or proposes different OPs"* ([VERIFIED] NIST). This is a *device* declining a *session* — it is the closest thing to consent in the amendment, and it is not consent: the person being measured is not a party to it.
- **Inference is out of scope.** *"Efficient signal processing algorithms (out-of-scope of IEEE 802.11bf) must be developed to accommodate low signal-to-noise ratio environment"* ([VERIFIED] NIST); *"the new standard is expected to leave the specific sensing algorithms open to implementation"* and *"IEEE 802.11bf is not expected to specify novel transmission schemes for radio signals"* ([VERIFIED] Meneghello, Chen, Cordeiro, Restuccia, IEEE Comms Mag 2023, unipd repository). The scope language stops at *"measurements that may be useful to estimate features of objects."* **The standard defines how cooperating stations exchange channel measurements. It defines nothing about what is inferred, by whom, about whom, or with what confidence.**

> **ROW P-6 — the D5 reading of P-1…1.2.** 802.11bf is a *measurement-exchange* standard: it makes CSI a negotiated, reportable MAC-layer object between consenting *devices*. Everything the consumer word "sensing" means — presence, motion, identity, vital signs — is an application layer the standard neither specifies nor bounds. In D5's form: the floor (who may measure whom, with what declared purpose and confidence, evidenced how) is MISSING from the standard, not inferior; a measurement layer without that floor is unsound for people-facing inference; a declared floor without measurement-honest evidence would be insufficient. No claim about our system is made here.

**Obstruction and range, as the literature actually uses the words.** "Through-wall" in verified papers means: one interior wall at ≤7 m (PhaseBeat); NLOS within a 6.2×3.5 m office (DeFall); 8–12 m and "one or multiple walls" for gait *speed* only (GaitWay); 18.5 m across five rooms with a *directional biquad antenna* on an ESP32-S3 (TU Wien); and, in KIT, no wall at all. Per-material attenuation numbers (drywall ≈1 dB; brick 6/15/15 dB; 203 mm concrete 29/48/54 dB at 2.4/5/6 GHz) are [REPORTED] via a secondary citing NIST 1997 — the NIST primary table was not fetched; treat the 6 GHz column as unverified. Vendor caveats agree with the physics: Comcast/Rogers: *"Devices directly above or below the gateway, on another floor, may be less effective"*; *"home size, layout, and building materials, may impact motion detection performance"* ([VERIFIED]).

**The training burden, stated plainly.** Every capability above binary motion is trained *somewhere* and degrades *elsewhere*: per-environment (HAR, fall, pose), per-user (ID, HAR), per-receiver (AGC — *"below 60% on every other device"* until normalised), per-position (gesture zone). The only capability with a credible zero-effort field record is motion detection with a centrally trained pet filter — and its published field number is 92.6 % detection with 8.4 % pet false alarms, not "almost zero."

**The CSI-access problem — what 11bf changes and what it does not.** *"CSI is not always available from the user side in commercial COTS Wi-Fi devices… most Wi-Fi chipset manufacturers keep CSI access as a private feature and do not provide a dedicated function interface to consumers"* (Du et al., [VERIFIED]); research still rides Intel 5300/AX210, Atheros, Nexmon-Broadcom and ESP32 tool-chains ([VERIFIED] CMC 2025 survey; CSIKit). Espressif's CSI is a vendor feature — the esp-csi README and ESP-IDF guide never mention 802.11bf ([VERIFIED]). 802.11bf standardises the *negotiated* exchange between cooperating stations; it does not by itself expose CSI to third-party apps on installed routers. The commercial products ship on vendor-private CSI: Cognitive on *"MaxLinear's suite of gateway and access platforms"* and partners *"Qualcomm, Broadcom, Onsemi, and MaxLinear"*; Origin *"captures and interprets this CSI data directly from the router"* ([VERIFIED]).

### 1.5 The envelope in one paragraph (for the docket)

Consumer Wi-Fi sensing, on 2026-08-30 evidence, robustly does one thing: detect that *something roughly person-sized moved* somewhere between a gateway and its stationary clients, whole-home, through interior walls, with a pet filter that leaves single-digit-percent false alarms — and the vendors selling it say so in their own disclaimers (no identification, no exact location, not a security service). Everything above that — activity, gesture, fall, breathing, counting, metric position — is real in the lab under a few-metre, mostly-LOS, single-or-few-person, same-environment, per-setup-trained regime and degrades by a third to a half when the room, user, or receiver changes. Identity is demonstrated in one room on plaintext beamforming feedback at 99.5 % and on CSI at 82.4 %, by a paper that says it may be overestimating; it is not demonstrated across rooms or days. Pose/imaging through walls is radar, not Wi-Fi. None of it is certified; none of it ships citing 802.11bf; and the standard that will eventually carry it defines the measurement handshake and nothing about the inference, the subject, or the consent.

### 2.4 The wedge's honest shape — what a consent-semantics layer would have to declare ([INFERRED], decision input, no design authority)

Synthesised from what the near-neighbours got right and what every vendor page omits. Ten declarations; each is a *field a layer would carry*, not a feature anyone is asked to build:

1. **Principal vs subject.** Account holder ≠ co-occupant ≠ minor ≠ guest ≠ neighbour ≠ passer-by. ETSI's definition binds to "the data subject"; 3GPP's to the subscriber; Comcast's to nobody but the account. The layer declares which class each field below binds to.
2. **Sensing volume.** Which spaces are inside the field: own dwelling, shared walls, adjacent units, the street. (Comcast advises sensitivity settings for shared-wall buildings; the physics — 8–15 m from outside an exterior wall (NDSS 2020) — makes the neighbour a subject by default.)
3. **Signal source and mode.** Active 11bf session (initiator/responder, declinable by the *device*) vs passive reception of others' plaintext BFI/CSI (KIT, LeakyBeam) — the second has no protocol-level refusal at all.
4. **Inference tier.** presence → motion → count → activity/gesture → vital signs → identity/gait → emotion. Each tier lands in a different legal box (MHMDA "vital signs"; GDPR Art. 9; AI Act Art. 3(41) RBI) — §2.5.
5. **Confidence and evidence.** The tier's honest error envelope (§1.4) travels with the inference, and each inference is attributable to a measurement, a model version, and an environment it was trained in. This is the row where a *physics-aware evidence layer* and a *consent layer* are the same object seen from two sides.
6. **Retention and locality.** Raw CSI/BFI vs derived events; on-device vs cloud; days (Comcast: 7).
7. **Disclosure.** To whom (law enforcement, litigants, "third parties"), on what process (subpoena vs warrant vs "emergency circumstances"), with what notice to the principal *and to the sensed non-principals*.
8. **Notice to non-principals and a refusal channel.** The camera-sign analogue for RF: an on-air or in-app declaration that sensing is active, and a per-person opt-out that does not route through the account holder (the Vodafone SMS pattern).
9. **Household governance.** Who may enable/disable; minors; the domestic-abuse case ("someone in your household set it up").
10. **Purpose lock.** Security vs wellness vs analytics vs insurance/advertising inference; secondary use forbidden by declaration, not by policy prose.

**Who is closest to owning it.** ETSI ISAC (vocabulary), 3GPP SA1 (normative gate), IEEE TGbf (the venue that declined), a security incumbent that now owns the leading sensing engine (ADT/Origin), and the two ISPs with the largest deployed base (Comcast/Cognitive; Rogers). None is positioned at the *home-orchestration* layer where the inference is composed with everything else the home knows — the same gateway observation RS-3 §6.6-2 made for enforcement. That is the honest shape of the wedge: the field is converging on needing a declaration object before anyone has named one, and the nearest vocabulary is in a 6G Group Report.

- 25(2): *"Where the category of machinery or related product is listed in Annex I, Part A, the manufacturer or the natural or legal person referred to in Article 18 shall apply one of the following procedures: (a) EU type-examination (module B) set out in Annex VII, followed by conformity to type based on internal production control (module C) set out in Annex VIII; (b) conformity based on full quality assurance (module H) set out in Annex IX; (c) conformity based on unit verification (module G) set out in Annex X."*
- 25(3): *"Where the category of machinery or related product is listed in Annex I, Part B, the manufacturer… shall apply one of the following procedures: (a) internal production control (module A) set out in Annex VI; (b) EU type-examination (module B)… followed by… (module C)…; (c) …(module H)…; (d) …(module G)…. If a manufacturer applies the internal production control procedure referred to in point (a), it shall design and construct the machinery or related product in accordance with the harmonised standards or common specifications specific to that category of machinery or related product covering all the relevant essential health and safety requirements. Where the category… is listed in Annex I, Part B, and the machinery or related product was not designed and constructed in accordance with the harmonised standards or common specifications… the manufacturer… shall apply one of the procedures referred to in points (b), (c) or (d) of this paragraph."*
- 25(4): *"Where the category of machinery or related product is not listed in Annex I, the manufacturer… shall apply the internal production control procedure (module A) set out in Annex VI."*

**The conformity-route difference, in one sentence:** Part A = third party always (B+C, H, or G — every route passes through a notified body); Part B = manufacturer self-assessment (module A) is allowed *only if* harmonised standards or common specifications covering all relevant EHSRs exist and were followed, else third party; unlisted = module A.

Therefore, the conformity assessment of a safety component or a system with self-evolving behaviour ensuring safety functions should be carried out by a third party, whether or not the safety component has been placed independently on the market or is part of a system embedded in machinery that is placed on the market.
