## §3 The letter — pages 2–3

**TRADEMARK SEARCH REPORT SUMMARY & ANALYSIS**

**Mark:** PALOKI

**lkClasses 9, 11, 42, and 45:** smart-home hardware and software

**Potential Conflicts with Registered Trademarks**

The purpose of a trademark is to identify where a particular good or service comes from. Before registering a mark on the Principal Register, the USPTO must evaluate whether any existing trademarks are likely to be confused with the one being applied for. The two main factors an Examining Attorney will evaluate are 1) the appearance of the two marks and 2) the relevant goods or services associated with each mark. However, this initial analysis may be outweighed by other relevant factors, including the differing commercial impressions created by the marks (meaning the impression consumers have upon seeing a mark in the marketplace) and the number of similar marks that have already been registered.

When determining whether certain goods and services are similar for the purposes of confusion, the USPTO typically does not solely consider whether goods or services are identical or overlapping, rather, it looks to whether they are related. Goods/services are usually considered “related” if there are many companies that produce both goods/services or categories of goods/services under the same mark. The more similar the goods/services are, the less similar the marks have to be to be confusing, and vice versa.

The search did not find any identical marks on the federal register. It did find a few registered marks that share some letters and sounds with PALOKI. We reviewed each one, and none appears to present a significant obstacle to registration in our opinion.

- **PALOCA** (Reg. No. 6301200)
  - **Class 9:** Batteries; Headphones; Loudspeakers; Microphones; Scales; Sunglasses; Blank USB flash drives; Cell phone cases; Computer keypads; ...
  - **Different ending/sound and different products.** The goods are batteries, audio, and phone accessories rather than smart-home systems. Owned by Yiwu Bailie Import and Export Company Limited (China).
- **PALOXY** (Reg. No. 6534030)
  - **Class 9:** Headphones; Microphones; Battery chargers; Capacitive styluses for touch screen devices; Covers for smartphones; Electronic pens; Headsets for cellular or mobile phones; Soundbar speakers; ...
  - **Distinct "-XY" ending and consumer audio and phone accessories.** The ending gives this mark a different sound and look. Owned by Shenzhen Yanchenghua Technology Co., Ltd. (China).
- **PULOKA** (Reg. No. 5708326)
  - **Class 9:** Cases for smartphones; Cell phone straps; Protective films adapted for smartphones
  - **Different first syllable and narrow goods.** Owned by Guangzhou Puloka Electronic Technology Co., Ltd. (China).
- **PALUKIA** (Reg. No. 7278245)
  - **Class 11:** Faucets; Showers; Sinks; Automatic faucets; Bidet taps; Faucet handles; Shower heads; Water control valves for faucets; Water purification installations; ...
  - **Different spelling/sound for plumbing fixtures; longer mark with a different ending..** Owned by Jiangmen Duojiabei Sanitary Ware Co., Ltd. (China).
- **PALAKI** (Reg. No. 7532082)
  - **Class 21:** Bottle pourers; Bottle squeegees; Bottles, sold empty; Containers for household or kitchen use not of precious metal; Plastic water bottles sold empty; ...
  - **Close spelling, but unrelated household containers.** Bottles and kitchen containers are different from smart-home hardware and software. Owned by an individual in Panama.

Based on these results, we believe there is a low possibility that the USPTO will refuse an application for PALOKI based on an existing registration. Each of the closest marks differs in sound or appearance, covers different products, or both. Keep in mind that each application is reviewed by an independent examining attorney, so no outcome is guaranteed.

**Potential Conflicts with Unregistered Trademarks**

Others who have been using similar, unregistered trademarks prior to your adoption of a name may be able to claim that you are infringing their rights by using your mark or filing an application for it at the USPTO. However, there are a few key considerations here, including who was using the mark first and how common the words in the mark are. The more widely the words are used, the less likely it is that infringement will be found.

The search also reviewed internet search engines, business directories, social media platforms, and online marketplaces for unregistered uses of PALOKI and similar terms. It turned up a small number of references. After reviewing them, we did not identify any unregistered use that appears likely to conflict with smart-home hardware or software.

Based on these results, we believe there is a low possibility that a third party using a similar mark without a registration would object to your use of PALOKI. Please note that no argument is an absolute defense, and none of this prevents someone from sending a demand letter or filing suit.

*Overall, based on the data available at the time of this search and the information you have provided, we believe there is a low possibility of a refusal to register PALOKI and a low possibility of an objection to its use. We recommend moving forward with an application.*

**Please let us know if you have any questions. If you would like to proceed, we can prepare and file your application for PALOKI.**
