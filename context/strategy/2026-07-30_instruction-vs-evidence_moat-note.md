# Instruction vs. Evidence — what model progress can and cannot absorb

## §1 The datapoint

On 2026-07-24 Anthropic published that it had removed **more than 80% of Claude Code's system prompt** for the Claude-5 generation of models, with no measurable loss on its evaluations. The primary source was fetched and verified in-session by the skills-currency-pass-2 lane on 2026-07-30 (`context/audits/2026-07-30_skills-pass-2_return.md` §7) against an independent digest; the headline claims check out.

Read plainly, that is a vendor deleting a large, carefully engineered harness because the model absorbed its function. The scaffolding did not fail. It stopped being necessary.

**This is the event the Substrate Thesis is built to avoid.** The thesis bets on occupying the layer that *compounds* with model progress rather than the layer that gets *consumed* by it (`Substrate_Thesis_v0.md`, the substrate bet). Here is a consumption event, in the open, at the frontier vendor, on a harness far better resourced than most. Anyone holding a position in the prompt-and-scaffold layer just watched the tide come in.

The thesis survives this — but only because of a distinction it has been carrying implicitly and has never stated in one line. That distinction is the point of this note.

## §2 The distinction

**Model progress absorbs instruction. It cannot absorb evidence.**

*Instruction* is text that tells a model how to behave — style, sequencing, decomposition, caution, format, the thousand small corrections that make a capable model act like a competent colleague. Instruction is compressible into weights, because that is precisely what training does. Every generation absorbs more of it. Any moat built from instruction is a moat with a known expiry date, and the expiry is set by someone else's release schedule.

*Evidence* is a proposition about the physical world that must be **observed** to be known. No amount of reasoning improvement tells a model whether the relay actually closed. That is not a reasoning deficiency to be trained away — it is an observation requirement. A model that is better at everything is still not a witness.

HomeSynapse's floor is an evidence mechanism, not an instruction mechanism. The pending-command ledger does not instruct anything. It withholds the word `CONFIRMED` until the device's own state report arrives, and says `UNCONFIRMED` when it does not. Model progress can make the proposer arbitrarily better; it cannot make the witness unnecessary.

This is the same shape as the enforcement position stated elsewhere in the strategy layer — *the harness enforces; the model only proposes* — but it supplies the missing **why**. The reason the enforcement layer is durable is not that determinism is virtuous or that models are untrustworthy. It is that enforcement here is a claim about the world requiring observation, and observation does not live in the weights.

## §3 What this does for the language law

The standing language law is that the deterministic floor is **MISSING** from the field, not **SUPERIOR** to what the field has (L2/L3 without L1 are unsound; L1 without L2 is insufficient). That law is correct and this note does not amend it. What this note adds is an explanation of *why the gap persists in plain sight*.

The field is making real, fast, legible progress on the instruction problem. Every absorption event — a shrinking system prompt, a better tool-use benchmark, a model that needs less hand-holding — reads as the harness problem closing. It is an easy inference and not an unreasonable one. But the two problems are unrelated. Each time instruction gets absorbed it *looks* like the enforcement gap narrowed, while the actuation-boundary evidence gap has not moved at all.

That reframes the competitive question usefully. The honest position is not "we are better than the agent platforms." It is: **the industry is solving a different problem well, and mistaking it for this one.** That is a claim we can make without disparaging anyone's work, and it is falsifiable — which is the next section.

## §4 Falsifiers

Named explicitly, so the position stays defeasible.

**F-1 (primary, Nick-named).** A model generation ships a **native, verifiable actuation-evidence mechanism** — not better tool-use reliability, not higher function-call accuracy, but a mechanism that makes a model's report of an external state change *independently checkable* without a separate witness. If that ships, §2's distinction collapses at the boundary that matters and the floor's un-retrofittability claim needs re-derivation from scratch. **Watch: agent- and tool-platform release notes, not model benchmark scores.** Benchmarks measure the instruction axis; this would appear on the platform axis.

**F-2 (secondary).** The enforcement layer proves **cheaply retrofittable**. If a competent team bolts a real confirmation ledger onto an existing platform in a quarter and it holds under hardware, then what we have is a lead, not a moat, and the strategy should be re-priced as a speed bet rather than a structural one. The Substrate Thesis already treats un-retrofittability as load-bearing; this note simply names the experiment that would kill it.

**F-3 (weak).** The absorption pattern reverses — harnesses grow again as models take on longer-horizon work. This would not refute §2 (which is analytic), but it would remove the rhetorical force of §1's datapoint, and the note should stop leaning on it.

## §5 What this does NOT establish — read this before quoting anything above

The argument in §2 is **analytic, not empirical**. It establishes that the evidence layer is not absorbable by better models. It establishes nothing whatsoever about whether anyone will pay for it. Demand is a separate question and remains the Substrate Thesis's §5-unproven item #2 (willingness-to-pay), untouched by anything here. A perfectly defensible position in a market that does not exist is still not a business.

Three further honesty constraints, held per the thesis's own §5/§9 discipline:

- The >80% figure is **first-party**, measured on **first-party evaluations**, with no per-category breakdown published. It is credible and it is not independent.
- **One datapoint is not a trend.** The force of this note is the distinction in §2, which stands on its own reasoning; §1 is the occasion for stating it, not the proof of it. If §1 turned out to be overstated, §2 would be unaffected.
- The north star's own standing caveat continues to apply to anything quoted externally: at least one load-bearing claim in that document is DESIGNED-FOR, not TRUE-TODAY. This note does not launder it.

## §6 Why this is filed rather than left in a transcript

Because the sentence in §2 is the most compressed true statement of the moat this project has produced, and the reason it is worth having in a file is the same reason the rest of the strategy layer is in files: **the repositories are the memory.** A formulation that lives only in a conversation is a formulation that has to be rediscovered, and rediscovery is not reliable.

Its intended uses are narrow and all downstream of the gate: it is the frame for the research lane's Charge 7 verification, a candidate framing for external positioning after the 2026-08-16 read, and the answer to give when someone reasonably asks why a better model does not make this unnecessary. **It moves no pre-freeze code.**

## ADDENDUM (2026-07-31) — three corrections from the agent-substrate research lane

The lane took §2 as a refutation target per Addendum 2's Charge 7. The distinction survived; three things about it sharpen. Source: `context/assessments/2026-07-28_agent-substrate-research_return.md` §8.4 (filed 2026-07-31).

**(1) The honest complication: witnesses exist. Custody does not.** §2 says a better model is still not a witness. True — but it understates what already ships. Matter's interaction model returns real device-side confirmation (invoke responses plus subscription-based reporting of actual state): a witness *statement*, from the device, which is materially more than the agent's say-so. What Matter does not provide is a **chain of custody** — those statements are confined to the fabric's session security, are not portably signed, and terminate in a controller log with no integrity guarantee. So the sharper claim is not that the witness is missing. It is that **the witness statement has nowhere to live.** The substrate's position is the custody chain over it: totally ordered, attributable, and — once chain-hashing lands (honesty table H3: DESIGNED-FOR, not TRUE-TODAY) — tamper-evident. Narrower, and considerably harder to refute.

**(2) The refutation attempt failed on shipping evidence, not on argument.** Tested against what platforms actually ship rather than announce: the MCP spec changelog carries no signed tool results, receipts, or attestation of any kind; OpenAI ships tracing, not verified execution receipts; A2A signs AgentCards (identity), not task results; Fetch.ai's AEVS and Microsoft's Verifiable Compliance Receipts both have the **agent signing its own report**. Self-attestation is not witnessing. The distinction stands on today's shipping evidence — and only on today's, which is why F-1 needs a watch rather than a conclusion.

**(3) F-1 gains a recognition rule.** The falsifier is a shipped, default-path mechanism in which the signature over an action's *result* is rooted **outside the agent stack** — the tool server, device, or counterparty signs what happened, such that a third party trusting neither the model nor its operator can verify the external state change. Anything where the actor signs its own report, however cryptographically dressed (including hash-chained self-logs and agent-keyed receipts), is a tool-use-reliability improvement and **not** F-1. Watch the platform axis: the MCP spec changelog and SEP stream, the A2A spec repo (signing extended from AgentCard to Task/Artifact), OpenAI's platform changelog, Matter spec releases, and the two leading indicators whose flip from draft/beta to shipped-default is the early warning — cMCP (`agentrust-io/cmcp`) and Microsoft's Verifiable Compliance Receipts. Fold into the existing quarterly watch; zero new standing cost.

**Priority: months, not years.** Two 2026 near-misses state adjacent halves of §2. Salfeld-Nebgen, "Governing Actions, Not Agents" (arXiv 2606.26298, June 2026) states the structural half almost exactly — correctness depending on a fact absent from the execution context cannot be adjudicated by alignment, monitoring, or sandboxing. AAIF's cMCP post (2026-07-29) states the trust-boundary half and ships TEE-signed per-call evidence. Neither states the **diachronic** half — that instruction is a substitutable input to a reasoner while evidence is a non-substitutable output of an observer — which the lane found genuinely unclaimed as of 2026-07-31. Convergence is visible. If this formulation is worth planting publicly, plant it in the first post-gate content window and cite both near-misses as the adjacent statements they are.

**§5 unchanged.** Willingness-to-pay remains untouched by all of the above; the lane's §8.3 verdict is that buyers demonstrably pay for evidence of events and nobody yet demonstrably pays extra for evidence about the evidence, absent a compelling authority. A perfectly defensible position in a market that does not exist is still not a business.

## ADDENDUM 2 (2026-07-31) — the downgrade event: evidence-layer honesty, one level deeper

Filed from the F-12 adjudication (pm-handoff v41 beat 2; carrier evidence in `nexsys-bench/scenarios/constants.yaml`, THE AUTO SUITE OF RECORD block).

A scenario asserting CONFIRMED failed twice and killed three theories. The root cause turned out to be two lines the platform had written **about itself** on 2026-07-19:

```
zigbee.reporting_configured: device=0x00124B002FA8D1C5 clusters=1 verified=0 degraded=1
zigbee.confirmation_downgraded: ... capability=on_off cluster=0x6
  posture=VERIFIED_REPORTS/SLEEPY outcome=best_effort
```

The system tried to configure attribute reporting on a device, could not verify it, and **lowered its own promise about that device before anyone asked it to keep one.**

That is this note's thesis at one further level of reflection. §2 says the substrate holds a witness statement that model progress cannot absorb. This says the substrate also **reasons about the reliability of its own evidence channel and discloses degradation prospectively.** The distinction matters more than it first reads: *a custody chain that does not know when its own inputs are degraded is a chain that launders uncertainty into false confidence.* The downgrade event is precisely the mechanism that stops the chain over-claiming — the difference between "we record what the device told us" and "we record what the device told us, and we told you in advance what that device's word is worth."

Every honest failure mode the field currently ships is **retrospective** — *that one didn't confirm.* This one is **prospective, per-device, and recorded before the command exists.** Flag-plant-content-grade for exactly that reason, and strictly stronger evidence for the position than any CONFIRMED pill.

It also supplies the answer to the obvious objection to §2. If the witness is unreliable, why is a custody chain over it worth anything? Because the chain records the witness's *known reliability alongside its testimony*. That is what a court does with an expert, and it is what no agent-governance stack currently does with a device.

**Charter: CONFIRM-SOUNDNESS** (launch-runway candidate) — per-device confirmation windows learned from the corpus (the false-UNCONFIRMED fix, with B4/B5 learned-tuning proposals as its data source, advisory-only and human-ratified inside AIOT-INV-1) · a scenario asserting the downgrade itself · the HUE-RESET re-shapes. The first exhibit is already banked: the corpus measured that one relay reports at ~11.6 s while its capability default assumes 5 s, and no spec sheet anywhere carries that number.
