# PELTON-READY — the ruling card (H10)

## §0 The rule on arrival
The letter outranks the day's plan on arrival (the 2026-08-28 card §1 stands). Nick saves it verbatim under `context/research/2026-09-<dd>_PELTON-OPINION_{{NAME}}_letter-verbatim.md` (the filing date; the name token in the filename, never the name), says back `ERIK: RETURNED <path> <bytes>` and ONE word from §1 — `CLEAR`, `CONDITIONS` or `NOTCLEAR` — and nothing else about the name in chat. The hub reads the letter at the bytes, confirms or corrects the class against §1's test, and rules the acts of §2 the same day. No act in §2 runs before the hub's ruling; the class word alone unlocks nothing.

## §1 The three classes and the test that assigns them (from the letter's own words)
| Class | The letter says (the test) | What it means for the program |
|---|---|---|
| **CLEAR** | no bar to registration found in the searched classes; risk characterized as low in every class we scoped; counsel recommends filing as scoped | the name is the name; the fence lifts through G-2's protocol, not by this word |
| **CLEAR-WITH-CONDITIONS** | a risk named in ≥ 1 class, or a recommended change to the scope, the identification language, a disclaimer, a class dropped, or a coexistence posture; counsel recommends filing WITH the change | the name survives; the identity is re-scoped before any outward act |
| **NOT CLEAR** | a likely refusal, a live conflict counsel advises against filing over, or a recommendation to choose another mark | the fallback runs; the fence stays; nothing outward carries the name |
A letter that mixes classes (clear in one class, adverse in another) is CLEAR-WITH-CONDITIONS if counsel still recommends filing in the remaining scope, NOT CLEAR if counsel does not. The hub assigns the class by quoting the sentence that decides it; the quote goes in the ruling, the name does not go in chat.

## §3 The results-consult questions (the call; one page; every class)
1. Which classes carry the risk, and what is the basis of each conflict — which likelihood-of-confusion factors the opinion weighed, and which mark(s) drive them?
2. For each risk: coexistence odds; the office-action posture; whether a knockout in one class re-scopes the filing rather than kills the mark.
3. The filing shape: §1(b) intent-to-use; owner NEXSYS LLC; the identification language counsel recommends per class; anything counsel wants changed in the identifications we proposed.
4. The specimen and statement-of-use timeline under §1(b) — the earliest and latest dates, and what counts as use for a software product sold to households.
5. The domain and the handles: whether securing them before the filing helps or exposes; whether any of them is evidence.
6. The fee and the filing date: can counsel file this week; what is needed from us by when.
7. On NOT CLEAR only: the turnaround for a comprehensive search on the next candidate, and whether a knockout-level screen from counsel precedes it.
8. The counsel wave (the 2026-08-28 card §3 item 5) — the two saved drafts (the written consent; the confirmatory IP assignment) for refinement at filing time.

## §5 What this card does not do
It does not grade the name; it does not lift the fence; it does not pre-decide the class. The letter's words decide; the hub quotes them in the ruling; Nick's word is the class, and `REVERT` is his if the ruling reads the letter wrong.
