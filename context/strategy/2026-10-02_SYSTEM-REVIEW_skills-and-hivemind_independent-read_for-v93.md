# The system, reviewed — skills and hivemind, v17 → v92

## §0 The verdict in one paragraph
The system works, and the discipline is the moat: every claim carries its instrument, every landing is gated, every lane returns to an audit, and the record is readable years later. The evidence is not ceremonial — a 95,000-line Java core with 3,445 tests and intact module boundaries, built by lanes that execute a 70 KB instruction with zero bounces, a review that found twelve defects before a line was written, a rig program that runs to an envelope. But the record has become the heaviest workload in the company. Seventy-six hub windows and roughly five hundred beats have produced 429 audits, 224 instructions and 170 hivemind commits in the last thirty days, while the core landed a handful of units; a beat averages 78 tool calls and 130 KB of written prose; the spine's byte caps are hit on every beat and trimmed by hand; the rulebook is ~250 KB and grows by one law per failure with nothing that retires one; the improvement register holds 119 rows of which 77 are OPEN and five are retired. The next phase is not more laws. It is structured state in place of hand-edited prose, sized lanes in place of maximum settings, a radar that ranks the backlog by instrument, and models used for what they are now good at — reading the real tree, planning, and parallel review — while the record keeps what it is good at: claims with instruments.

## §1 What the system got right (keep, unchanged)
1. **The instrument beside the claim.** The premise gate (law #32) and the two-layer audit turned authoring from opinion into reads. In v89 the instruction's §0b carried fourteen greps beyond the pre-verification's eighteen; the independent review re-ran them and found ten citation misses and two design gaps (the IAS re-learn, the null `deviceId`) — each one a bounce the lane did not have to make. The lane re-ran all thirty-two rows in minutes and wrote 1,806 lines that passed `check` on the first attempt.
2. **The commit boundary.** Every repo is Nick's hands; the hub writes trees and hands gated cards. Zero accidental pushes, zero mystery commits, every landing with a census and a message that reads as a record. The IR-101 gate (the card refuses on a count mismatch or a trailer hit) was the right fix for the one slip.
3. **The lesson loop.** Seventy-five lessons and sixty-eight ledger arcs — the system does learn from each failure, and the lessons are specific (the grant before the paste; the restore before the gap; the tool's own validator at the cut).
4. **The envelope and the board.** THE FORTNIGHT gave a critical path with dates and a rig envelope the sittings respected; windows close on a deliverable; v91 was folded when v90 finished early — the cadence adapts.
5. **The cloud form with its exit.** Three cloud lanes (PJ-2, BH-3, IR-67) came home on branches with returns and PRs; the hub audited them at the bytes in its own clone. This is the lane runtime that scales.

## §2 What it now costs (the honest part, with the numbers)
1. **The record is the bottleneck, not the code.** v89: the instruction 70 KB, the review 14 KB, two audits ≈ 15 KB, the spine edits ≈ 40 KB, the DR 17 KB — ≈ 150 KB of hub prose and about six hours of hub time for one unit the lane executed in 47 minutes. Across the record: 9 MB written in the 70 beats that tracked it. The hub spends its context on describing work more than on deciding it.
2. **The spine machinery is brittle and the caps are fought by hand.** Four of my own splices needed three to five re-runs for a 48-byte snapshot overage, a 152-byte brief overage, and a trailer assert that trips on files that lawfully quote the gate's grep string. A splice damaged the register at v90 b6 (repaired at v92 b1; `splice_lib_v2`). The brief sat at 99 % of its cap all day on 10-02 (D-v92-28 c). Every re-run is a device round trip and a new file name. None of this moves the product.
3. **The dialect taxes every fresh reader.** The record's prose is a private language — the FORM, the word, at the bytes, the recs by silence, THE WHOLE-PASTE LAW — and the laws live in four places (SKILL.md §3, the ledger, the dispatch texts' law paragraphs, pm-lessons) joined by "read by range". The 45 KB boot read is the most expensive 45 KB in the system because it is compressed back-reference, not state. A compaction keeps a skill's first 5,000 tokens; the PM SKILL.md is ≈ 5,500 — the laws section is the part that falls off.
4. **The register is a backlog, not a radar.** 119 rows, 77 OPEN, five retired, no age, no cost-of-ignoring, no owner lane. Rows are minted at every intake and retired only at a Phase 2; nothing ranks them, so they cannot drive priorities — the thing Nick most wants the system to do.
5. **The operator cadence is the throughput ceiling.** Every landing waits for a card in Nick's hands; BC7 bundled six parts and its first STOP stranded five; a hand dry-run missed a U+2014 in a regex. The boundary is right (trust, auditability); the card size and the hand dry-run were not. D-v92-28 (a) and (b) already rule the fixes — one instrument per card; `card_dryrun_v1` as a tool.
6. **Cost is not sized to the task.** `$409` → `$145` in six hours with no hub-dispatched lane running, then `$16` by morning; DOCS-1 ran a 32-agent fan-out for 2 h 50 min to land a 10-file, +62/−8 docs change. The dispatch named maximum settings; nothing sized the lane to the diff. A docs change of sixty lines is one agent at ordinary effort.
7. **The field lags the record.** The product lives on the Pi and in the house: the Hue has been dark for two days (IR-112), the SNZB-06P24 left the network in five frames (IR-117), the nightly and boot-health cannot see a dark device (IR-118), the window's close is never logged (IR-119), the serial link rejects frames on CRC (IR-120). The system writes 130 KB per beat about process while it cannot see that a device went dark. The soak on Wednesday would run on 8 of 10.
8. **The market layer has words, not instruments.** `OUTREACH:`, `TM:`, `DRAFT:` are owed words on Nick's calendar; 110 strategy files, and no measurement of whether the market is moving — a pilot household's yes is the only one that counts, and nothing in the record tracks the funnel to it.

## §3 How the models changed what the hub should do
1. **Fable 5.1 at the highest effort executes a long spec flawlessly and re-verifies it faster than the hub wrote it.** The lane re-ran thirty-two premise rows before its first write and stopped nowhere. The implication: the hub should write INTENT, INVARIANTS and the PREMISE TABLE (≤ 15 KB), and let the lane produce the Files table and the tests as a PLAN return (ten to fifteen minutes, cheap) that the review reads against the real tree before GO. The model with the repo in front of it writes a better Files table than the hub grepping from a container. Keep the one-way-door review and the premise gate — they earned their place; move the plan to the side that can see the tree.
2. **A same-family reviewer finds many defects; a different family finds different ones.** The v89 review (Fable reviewing Fable) found twelve, all real. Cross-family review would catch what both share as blind spots and miss some of what one family sees — so for one-way doors use both, for ordinary instructions one. This is the strongest argument for Nick's split.
3. **Multi-agent runs are a cost multiplier before they are a capability.** DOCS-1's 32 agents are the exhibit. A charter must carry a budget line (`CREDITS-CAP: $n`, agents ≤ n) and the return's §0 states the spend; the hub sizes the lane to the diff. Multi-agent earns its cost on breadth (research across sources, parallel review of independent seams, a fleet of greps), not on a sixty-line change.
4. **Compaction is a design constraint on skills.** The invariants must live in a skill's first 5,000 tokens; everything else is a playbook loaded for the task at hand (the coding-instruction playbook, the bench-card playbook, the cloud-dispatch playbook, the Phase-2 playbook). The current SKILL.md is a rulebook with a state pointer; it should be a short charter with pointers to playbooks.

## §4 What to change — the recommendations (each refutable by Nick's word)
- **R1 — Structured state.** Replace the hand-edited digest and brief with a small `context/status/state.yaml` (lanes, words owed, shas, risks, the next act) that the hub EDITS, and a script that RENDERS the snapshot and the brief from it. The byte caps vanish because the prose is generated; the boot reads 2 KB of state + the newest DR section; the splice library becomes a CLI with named operations (`bank-word`, `append-beat`, `rotate`, `render`) instead of a bespoke 300-line script per beat. This is D-v92-28 (c) taken to its end.
- **R2 — The plan-return lane.** For every coding unit: the hub cuts intent + invariants + the premise table; the lane returns a PLAN (the Files table, the tests, the counts) and WAITS; the review reads the plan; GO; the lane executes. The instruction shrinks from 70 KB to ≤ 15 KB; the review reads a plan built on the real tree; the lane's execution is unchanged.
- **R3 — A law budget.** Cap the standing invariants at twenty, in the skill's first 5,000 tokens; every new law retires or merges one; the sixty-eight arcs become the playbooks' exhibits, not boot reads. The dialect is translated once into plain terms in a glossary the fresh reader loads first.
- **R4 — The radar.** The register gains `age`, `cost-of-ignoring` (1–3) and `owner lane`; a weekly radar pass (an Opus Max multi-agent lane fits: breadth, parallel reads) ranks the OPEN rows and the open risks against the critical path and proposes ≤ 5 re-prioritizations in H10 form, each with the instrument that would confirm it. Its inputs are a `METRICS.md` the hub never types by hand: lanes dispatched and landed per week, credits per landing, sittings planned versus held, devices on the air versus in the registry, register in versus out, CI green rate, the funnel to a pilot yes.
- **R5 — Model allocation (Nick's proposal, adopted with bounds).** Fable 5.1 (Extra) stays the hub: the spine's single writer, the one that holds the dialect and the laws. Opus 5.5 Max multi-agent takes four jobs, each as a chartered lane with a budget line and a filed return: (i) the independent review of one-way doors and instructions (beside a Fable reviewer for one-way doors; alone for ordinary units); (ii) the long-horizon research charter (RESEARCH-LH); (iii) the weekly radar and the strategy re-cuts (THE WEEKS AHEAD, THE HORIZON); (iv) a second-check of THE HORIZON tonight, after v93 cuts it. Not every beat, not every card: the second-checker is bound to the record — it greps the same premises and writes file:line findings — or it produces opinions.
- **R6 — The field first.** Before the soak: the dark-device detector in the nightly and boot-health (IR-118), the window-close log line (IR-119), the CRC trend as a health signal (IR-120), the dark Hue adjudicated (IR-112). Small units, high leverage; THE HORIZON puts "the bench can see every device" ahead of any new Java feature. The product is the house that works.
- **R7 — The operator.** Keep the commit boundary. Cards ≤ 3 parts, tool-checked (decided). Add a scheduled daily heartbeat (a cloud session that only READS — the nightly, CI, the register, the fleet line — and writes a 1 KB `HEARTBEAT.md`; no cards, no spine edits) so each hub window starts with the facts and never asks `NIGHTLY:` again.
- **R8 — Prose-to-code ratio as a metric.** Track hivemind bytes written per core line landed, weekly. It is the honest measure of whether the record serves the product; the target direction is down.
