## §4 One page — what the pilots feed back into the system (the hub's account; for Nick's read, not for the household)
**§4.1 The record is the product's own evidence, and the pilot is the first record not made by the builder.** Every claim the company may say in public is a register row: dated, commit-hashed, evidence-pointed, fenced (the claim register; H9). The rows so far (C-001..C-003) were minted on Nick's rig. A pilot household produces the first evidence the builder did not stage: the event store on that Pi — every command, every confirmation, every availability transition, every automation run with its trigger and its verdict — plus the nightly digest and the recovery record. From it the next register rows mint at the same bar as the first three: **C-004** (the packaged build runs a week in a household other than the builder's; the fleet re-adopted on that Pi; `issues=0` at its boots) and **C-005** (an automation fired in that house and its confirmation came back from the device) — each with its commit hash and its refutable-by, each said narrow.

**§4.2 What flows back, by surface.**
- *The weekly five-minute conversation* → the household sentences. DRY2-PASS's five sentences (the plan review §6.4) are the pre-registered form of what a household says about a week with the system ("the light came on when I came in; it did not come on when the dog did"). A household's own sentence that the record can confirm is the strongest outward material the company has — and the only kind the register admits.
- *"Why did it fire / why didn't it / did it actually confirm"* → the explain surface's gaps. Every question the household asks that the dashboard cannot answer in its own words is an improvement-register row (the IR-95 class: the automation that "succeeded" while commanding nothing is exactly the kind of gap a household finds in a day that a bench finds in a month).
- *Device deaths and silences* → honest-failure exhibits (W-3). A device that dies in the pilot and is reported UNKNOWN by the silence gate — never manufactured-ALIVE — is a manufactured exhibit of the C-2 availability position on hardware the builder did not pick.
- *The install itself* → the reproducible-install claim (W-2). A second Pi, installed from the same `.deb` by the same card, in a house the builder does not live in, is the install-fragility class measured instead of assumed; PKG-FRESH-1b (Wed Oct 14) rehearses exactly this on the bench first.
- *The run's volume* → the kit's storage decision (`VOLUME:` and `KIT-STORAGE:` after dry-run #1's F1 count): a household's per-device-per-day event count sets R-H's constant; two households set its spread.

**§4.3 What does NOT flow — the posture the household is told in one sentence.** Nothing leaves the house on its own. The record lives on the Pi; the weekly copy to Nick's desk is a copy the household hands over (the consent sentence in §3), used to read the week and mint rows — never to build a profile, never to sell, never to train anything. No sensing (presence, occupancy, radio sensing) is part of a pilot: the four-qualifier law forbids the sentence and the privacy posture (E-4) is not yet a written surface, so the presence dataset (the FOP Phase 2) does not begin with these two households. PILOT-TELEMETRY-1 — what, if anything, is collected beyond the household's own copy — is a post-run decision (D-v107's company row) and is not promised either way; to the household the word is "the record stays in your house; once a week you let me read a copy".

**§4.4 What the pilots change in the plan.** A YES by Wed Oct 14 puts the household's onboarding card on the calendar after the run (the pilot Nov 25; PI-2's second Pi Oct 20 is its hardware). Two YESes make the Dec 1–14 re-read a read of two records, not one — and that re-read is COMMERCIAL-1's decision point (nothing commercial before the written opinion and the pilot's record). A NO is banked and the next candidate is Nick's choice; three NOs re-read this script before a fourth call.
