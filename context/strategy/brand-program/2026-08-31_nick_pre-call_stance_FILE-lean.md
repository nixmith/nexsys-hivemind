# Nick's pre-call stance (verbatim, 2026-08-31)

**The bottom line.** Yes — on this record I believe you can take ZENDOMO and file it, provided tomorrow's call clears the two questions your email already asks (the two-entity read and the DOMO family). My lean isn't "file because the risk is zero." It's "file because the risk is bounded, priced, mostly on the recoverable axis, and — critically — shrinking is not what it does with time. Waiting makes it worse."

**The registration axis** (can you get the certificate) is where the real friction lives; a 2(d) citation of Zendo Inc.'s ZENDO is the EXPECTED case, not the bad case — but the response position is unusually strong: the Office itself registered ZENDO (8398495) over the already-registered ZENDO (6961839) in overlapping class-42 software space thirteen days ago; the crowd (≥106 distinct live ZEN-marks in 9/42 as a floor); LOCKIN's standalone DOMO published with no office action over two other registered class-9 DOMOs; identifications drawn hard to local-first device-control OS language. Worst realistic case: a response cycle or two, thousands in fees, months of calendar — the recoverable axis by definition.

**The use axis** (can anyone force a rename after launch) is the feared axis and the most reassuring on evidence: a forced rename requires a federal suit and an injunction (~$200k+ to the plaintiff through discovery). Zendo Inc.: interior-design renderings, ~50 downloads, stalled since January, let its own companion application die under one office action — not a plaintiff's behavioral profile. ASARUM: closest goods but no US registration found, US use in months at ~500-download scale, a UK micro-consultancy's balance sheet. Deep AI: different universe. The DOMO owners: a crowd whose existence proves nobody owns the element. Nobody on this board shows propensity + rights position + war chest at once. The cheap vector (an app-store complaint) reaches only the future companion app and is answerable with a filing receipt and a goods distinction.

**The honest scenario:** ASARUM's trajectory over 2–4 years — tiny but alive, shipping weekly, official Homey partner, in the neighboring ecosystem. If they become "the floorplan app everyone knows" and then file in the US, the marketplace picture gets uncomfortable. That scenario is the single strongest argument for filing NOW: a §1(b) filing date beats any later application of theirs; their common-law rights stay bounded by actual US reputation (~500 downloads, four months); four new ZEN-/DOMO- filers landed in this neighborhood in twelve months — every week of waiting risks flipping us from senior applicant to junior. The §1(b) priority date is the one asset use can't buy and waiting can't recover.

**Zen/domotics:** legally useful in a limited way — the coined-compound commercial-impression argument plus the weak-element argument (the register already partitions ZEN-space by field-of-use carve-outs Zendo Inc. drafted unprompted). A supporting beam, not the foundation: examiners weigh appearance and sound heavily, and ZENDOMO contains ZENDO letter-for-letter.

**"Filing confidently" in practice:** file 9+42 only, IDs drawn to local-first home-automation OS/device control, zero design/layout/interior vocabulary · get counsel's confirmation on the two-entity read (if linked, or if enforcement facts exist off the public record, LET the picture change) · house mark forward on the eventual companion-app listing · a watch service after filing + a quarterly thirty-second glance at both Zendo apps' store pages (traction drift = the propensity tripwire) · consent contact held in reserve as the cure for a refusal, never a pre-filing move · expect the office action without flinching. Permanent expectation: no mark is unassailable — VERDOMO carries the same DOMO tail plus its own unknowns. The question was never "is ZENDOMO clean" but "is its risk bounded, priced, and better than the alternatives' risk plus the cost of switching." On everything verifiable: yes. The composite entity in the letter's scariest sentence does not exist as one company; split into its two real halves, neither half justifies abandoning the name.
