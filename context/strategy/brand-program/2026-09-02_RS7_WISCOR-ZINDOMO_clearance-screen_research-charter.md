# RS-7 — the successor-name screen: WISCOR · ZINDOMO (evidence at the public primaries)

## §0 The frame the lane serves (do not re-litigate) — the Pelton method, as we learned it
1. **Two axes, graded separately.** The RIGHTS/REGISTRATION axis (can the Office register it; can a senior user oppose or cancel) and the USE/SHUTDOWN axis (can anyone force a rename after launch). Registration failure is recoverable (fees, months); use-stoppage is the feared axis. Both get a grade per candidate; neither substitutes for the other.
2. **First-to-USE, not first-to-file.** US priority flows from use in commerce. A senior user with NO US registration still holds rights where it has US reputation — and "prices in USD on the site + US store downloads" counts as use directed at our market (the call's ASARUM lesson). For every hit: WHEN did US use start, HOW MUCH (downloads, ratings, revenue signals), and is it GROWING.
3. **Blocking vs coexistence-manageable, mark by mark.** A hit is graded on DuPont factor 1 (sight · sound · meaning · commercial impression, each stated), factor 2 (goods/services relatedness — same channel? same buyer?), and the crowd (how many live marks share the element; a crowded field weakens each and helps coexistence). Say which it is; say why.
4. **Enforcement propensity + shutdown-vector ranking** (RS-6 §4 — reuse, do not re-derive): platform complaint (cheap; reaches a companion app) > TTAB (registration-only) > federal suit (the true stop-use vector; ~$200k+ to a plaintiff) > UDRP. For each material hit: size, funding, TTAB history (TTABVUE), dispute footprint, counsel of record.
5. **The ID scope we will file:** cl. 9 + 42 only, drawn to a local-first home-automation OS / device-control / event-log software; NO design/layout/interior vocabulary; alarm/lock language phrased with care. Secondary classes to SCREEN (not file): 11 (home devices), 35, 38. Owner: NexSys LLC (Louisiana). Basis: §1(b) intent-to-use.
6. **Strength of the mark itself.** Coined/fanciful > arbitrary > suggestive > descriptive. State where each candidate sits and WHY (dictionary + foreign-language check; is "WISCOR" a word, a surname, a place, an acronym anywhere?).

## §1 The questions — falsifiers first, per candidate (WISCOR first; ZINDOMO second; the same rows)
1. **Knockout at the USPTO** (the public trademark search — disclose which UI/instrument was reachable): exact match; phonetic equivalents; variant spellings — for WISCOR: WISCORE · WISKOR · WHISCOR · WISKER · WHISKER · WISECORE · WISE CORE · VISCOR · WISCO/WISCOR-formatives; for ZINDOMO: ZINDOMO · ZINDO · ZIN DOMO · ZENDOMO (our own prior clearance) · the ZENDO family (Reg. 8398495; 6961839) · the standalone-DOMO family (LOCKIN sn 99001229; SKYLRK; Domo Inc.; NHK/Linea). FALSIFIER (WISCOR): a live cl-9/42 registration or application for WISCOR/WISCORE/a homophone in software/connected devices → the coined-name advantage is gone. FALSIFIER (ZINDOMO): the examiner-facing sight/sound overlap with ZENDO is graded by the lane as likely-citable → it inherits ZENDOMO's defect.
2. **THE WHISKER ROW (WISCOR).** The hub's UNVERIFIED recollection: WHISKER is a live US brand in connected home-pet devices (an app + hardware; the Litter-Robot maker). Establish at TSDR + the brand's own site + both app stores: what marks, what classes, what goods, how big, any enforcement history. Then grade DuPont-1 sound similarity WIS-cor vs WHIS-ker honestly (a native English speaker's ear; two syllables, matching stress, differing vowel + coda) and factor-2 relatedness (pet devices with a home app vs a home-automation OS: same channel? same buyer?). 3. **Common-law and foreign surfaces (both candidates).** Google Play + Apple App Store (exact + near names; ratings counts; last-update dates; developer country; currencies shown) · GitHub/npm/PyPI (a "wiscor" package or org) · Crunchbase/OpenCorporates/Companies House/US state SOS registries (any company named WISCOR / Wiscor Inc./LLC/Ltd — where, what, alive?) · EUIPO + UKIPO + WIPO Global Brand Database (a foreign registration can claim §44 priority in the US — the ASARUM lesson in reverse) · social handles (@wiscor on X/Instagram/TikTok/YouTube — held? by whom? active?) · domain: wiscor.com WHOIS status + archive.org history (was it ever a business? when did it lapse?), plus the variant domains (wiscore.com, wiscor.io/.ai/.app) — owned by whom.
4. **The crowd arithmetic.** LIVE WIS-/WISE-formatives in cl. 9/42 and LIVE -COR/-CORE-formatives in cl. 9/42 (floors acceptable; disclose truncation) — for ZINDOMO reuse RS-6's ZEN- and -DOMO counts, refreshed only if cheap. 5. **Foreign-language + connotation screen (both).** Any meaning, slang, or unfortunate homophone in Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Italian, Japanese, Mandarin, Hindi, Arabic (the markets a home-OS could reach). "cor" (heart — Latin/Catalan/Portuguese) and "wis/wise" are the expected reads; find anything worse.
6. **The brand-taste screen (both; scored, not felt).** A stated rubric, each row 1–5 with a sentence: pronounceability from print · spellability from sound (the dictation test: say it to three imagined listeners — how many spell it right?) · memorability · fit to the positioning (local-first, deterministic, explainable — "why did it fire?") · warmth vs crispness for a HOME product · wordmark/lockup potential (letterforms; the katakana test from B-7) · the industrial/B2B misread risk (does it sound like a holding company?).
