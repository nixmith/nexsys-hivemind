Nick's two corrections stand, at the source: (i) a rename is not a find-and-replace — the namespace string and the migration bytes would each break every existing install while CI stayed green (the tests derive ids through the same function; the migration checksum halts the boot); (ii) the company name does not block the trademark, and it does block the public release unless the file headers are separated from the entity first.

## §3 The read on the names (honest; both sides)
### §3.1 The product name — build it, as planned
The record's ruling stands and this read does not reopen it. What the input adds that matters: the wordmark must never split at the L (the earlier screen's argument against Grafana's LOKI mark rests on one invented word, and the dashboard sits beside logs); pick the GitHub organization-name variant the day the application is filed; confirm the `.com`'s registrant is the LLC with 2FA and auto-renew on — Nick's own hands, one hour, with `TM:` (D-v83-13).
### §3.2 The legal entity — NexSys is a private name with a shelf life; the fix is to make the code not care
Investor side: nobody funds a legal name; they fund the product and the founder. But an investor's counsel will find six live NEXSYS registrations in a diligence search and ask why the LLC carries a name it can never use in public — a question with a cost (hours, a rename mid-diligence) and no upside. Customer side: the household never needs the entity's name until it appears on an invoice, a privacy policy or an App Store seller line — and then it should be the name they already know. **The move the input recommends is right and cheap: separate the name from the code NOW** — SPDX headers with either `Copyright The {{NAME}} Authors` (the Linux Foundation form) or no per-file line at all (Apache's own policy keeps it in NOTICE) — so the entity's name lives in at most one file and the November release carries no 1,230-file dependency on a company name. Then **rename the LLC in place at the first trigger** (an App Store listing · the public install flow or first sale · a standards-body membership · the first investor conversation), never by forming a new company: an intent-to-use application assigned to anyone but a successor to the ongoing business is void, so the applicant of record must be the entity that files the statement of use. The counsel question, verbatim for the attorney-search draft: *"Confirm that an in-place name change of the Louisiana LLC (same EIN) preserves the ITU application filed in the LLC's current name, and the recording steps at the USPTO."*
### §3.3 The parent — the hub's opinion: do not put Asimtote on the paperwork; rename the LLC to the product's name at the trigger, and keep the parent as a held option
**For Asimtote (the case its defenders make, and it is a real case):** the company's long line is accountability infrastructure for other people's intelligence — the north star, the Substrate Thesis, the research tracks — and that audience reads names more than it hears them; a parent name distinct from the home product keeps the company from being defined by its first market; the field's own precedent is exact (Nabu Casa above Home Assistant, and the Open Home Foundation move was clean *because* the names differed); the screens are clean today and the `.com`/`.io` are held; the vertical-asymptote reading (the barrier function at the edge of the safe set — the L1 story) is a good story for the formal-methods reader.
**Against it, and why the hub lands here:** (a) **Stage.** A two-name architecture is two marks to clear, two handle sets, two stories, and every early document (the terms, the invoices, the seller line) shows the household a name it has never heard. A one-founder company entering its first pilot should carry one name; the parent arrives when the second business exists (the Alphabet pattern is a *later* move, and Nabu Casa was founded to hold an existing project, not to launch one). (b) **The name itself carries costs the input measured:** three pronunciations in play (the dictionary's, and two the record has used); it reads as a misspelling to exactly the technical reader it is aimed at (Romanian *asimptote*, Turkish/Indonesian *asimtot*); the GitHub handle is a person's and the PyPI name is a live Cambridge tool using the same metaphor; it has never been cleared, and Asimptote B.V. — one letter away, in software — is the question counsel would have to answer before it went on anything. A name that needs a footnote to land is a cost for a company that will spend the next twelve months talking to households. (c) **Investor side, plainly:** an investor hears a parent name at the seed stage as either ambition or diffusion; with one product and one pilot the evidence supports focus, and focus is the asset being sold. **Customer side:** the household wants one word for the thing in their house and the people behind it. **Therefore:** the recommendation is `COMPANY-NAME: product` — the LLC becomes the product's name at the first trigger (in place, same EIN); Asimtote stays what the record made it in July, the quiet parent, as a *held option* — the domains kept, the July clearance brief kept unsent until a second product line or a holding structure exists — and if Nick's conviction is that the substrate business *is* the company and the home product only its wedge, then `COMPANY-NAME: parent` is defensible **only after** the three checks (the pronunciation study of ten and ten; a proper knockout search and Pelton's quote on the July brief; one handle variant everywhere) and a fixed pronunciation — and still not before the trigger. **Refutable-by:** a term sheet, a partner or a standards body that requires the company to be named for the platform rather than the product before the pilot ends.
### §3.4 What the hub would not do
Rename the knowledge base or the private repos (history is never rewritten; the codenames cost nothing a customer sees); touch the hash namespace or a migration byte for any reason; put any candidate name in an outward text before its protocol row; let the November header rewrite touch V001–V005 (the checksum halt); spend counsel's hours on the parent before the pilot has a household.
