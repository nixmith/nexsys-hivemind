## 3. Findings of record (F-P1..F-P7)

- **F-P1 (the bottom line, verbatim-class):** *"Overall, our analysis is that there is a moderate to high risk that the USPTO would cite another mark against yours and refuse registration and/or find that it is a weaker surname trademark… we do not recommend moving forward with an application to register TAMORO at the USPTO, or using the proposed mark, given the elevated risk that it is not approved for registration and the risk that OpenAI/Tomoro AI Ltd will be alerted to your application and try to prevent you from using it."* Counsel recommends against BOTH registration AND use — the full adverse branch, not conditional-clear.
- **F-P2 (the §2(d) core):** "rather likely" that TAMORO draws a 2(d) refusal on TOMORO (relatedness of goods/services + similar appearance, sound, meaning). The available response (diluted TOMORROW-formative field + mark differences) is an after-refusal argument with no assurance — "each case is different and decided by an independent Examiner."
- **F-P3 (the conflict stack is FOUR-deep, not one):** beyond TOMORO — TASORO (reg., Cl 11 overlap with our tier-2 class), TAMOP (reg., Cl 9 smart-home wording; apparently unused — but "cancellable" is a proceeding, not a clearance), ALTAMORO ×2 (fresh 2026 Cl 11 ITUs). "Moderate-high chance that the USPTO would cite any or all."
- **F-P4 (surname):** TAMORO is a rare surname; low-moderate risk of a §2(e)(4) primarily-merely-a-surname refusal → Supplemental Register fallback = a weaker registration. This risk STACKS on F-P2 independently.
- **F-P5 (the alert cost is asymmetric):** filing is a public act that alerts an OpenAI-owned senior with active prosecution; "OpenAI is litigious against trademark applications." The application itself creates the adversary contact.
- **F-P6 (the mitigation is incompatible with the product):** counsel's if-you-proceed-anyway mitigations are a logo-form filing + "a more limited scope of goods and services, avoiding any goods or services for 'automation' or 'AI'." **A home-automation operating system whose identification cannot say "automation" has no honest identification** — the mitigation gives up the goods the mark exists to cover. This finding, more than any probability, is what kills the proceed-anyway branch.
- **F-P7 (use-without-filing is not an exit):** the no-use recommendation covers common-law adoption too — use without registration keeps the confusion exposure, adds willfulness exposure once on notice, and forfeits the registration-based deterrents. A trust-infrastructure brand cannot sit on a mark it can neither register nor defend.
